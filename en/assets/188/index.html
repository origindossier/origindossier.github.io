Maja Petrović: selected statements from Vera’s dossier used to defend adoption — CSR submission dated 22 December 2025

CSR archive submission P2.1435/24 — 22 December 2025

Full translation of the submission on the first two pages of the supplied 15-page file. Its heading and closing date are 22 December 2025; the court receipt stamp is dated 24 December 2025. The new public PDF contains these two submission pages. The historical attachments are identified below and linked to the existing dossier. The source’s party labels, quotations, dates and name variants are preserved.

That the relatives did not want to assume care of the child can clearly be concluded from the narrative of the statement of claim on page 4, which states:

Existing historical attachments and source boundaries

Pages 3–15 of the supplied file correspond to physical pages 1–13 of the already archived Vera Radovanović dossier 3871/79: the cover and inventory, the decision dated 16 April, the three-page foster-family questionnaire, the two-page agreement dated 25 April, four contact-monitoring sheets and the front of the personal record. The existing complete 14-page PDF and confirmed English reading remain available.

The supplied annex set does not include the personal record’s reverse, which is physical page 14 of the existing dossier. Nor is a separate copy of the listed decision Up-3-356/79 of 5 July 1979 visible among those thirteen scans. This describes the supplied file, not what was or was not filed with the court. The monitoring-sheet entry about delivery on 5 July does not by itself identify that particular decision.

Editorial notes on the printed submission

The heading again labels CSR as plaintiff and Milan Petrović as defendant; the body and signature describe CSR as the defendant. These labels are preserved. The submission attributes its quoted passage to 6 April; the existing confirmed transcription places that passage under 8 April. The confirmed handwriting transcription has not been changed. The printed name Vizmet is a variant of the already identified Julka Vizmeg.

Later author’s note

The author regards this submission as crowning evidence supporting his case and describes his joy on reading it. He believes that Maja selected the parts she thought served her position, expecting him not to decipher Vera’s handwriting. This is his interpretation of their intention, separate from the words of the submission. The comparison remains available through the existing, previously confirmed transcription of Vera’s notes.

Omitted records of a healthy and well-cared-for child

                
On the same page of Vera’s dossier as the quoted passage, the entry of 8 April describes the child as physically and mentally normally developed, healthy, well cared for and clean. Probate representation is not a decision about child care

                
Maja Petrović infers that the relatives did not want to assume care from the lawyer’s withdrawal. Helena Uzelac’s original letter of 3 April concerns mutually agreed termination of representation of the specific heir Roža Kopljan. That is a lawyer–client relationship in probate, not a declaration by all relatives about care. The probate record of 19 March places the child with the late Pál’s parents and proposes a guardian to protect his interests in the estate. The appeal about legal paternity must be read within that procedural setting, separately from actual family care.

Unestablished legal paternity and a known natural father

                
The submission refers to unestablished paternity and Pál allegedly claiming to be the father, then quotes the natural father’s death and identifies his sister. Formal judicial establishment of paternity differs from knowledge and acknowledgment of a biological father. In divorce case P.1642/76, Pál personally says he had a son with Cvita, while his wife Vera Lakatoš refers to his son with another woman; the probate record also notes his acknowledgment of the child alongside the absence of judicially established paternity. These specific records cannot be reduced to unknown parentage merely because legal registration remained unresolved.

An old stigmatizing assertion is not proof of paternity

                
In 2025, CSR quotes Vera’s assertion that Cvita lived with several men to support an assessment that Pál’s paternity was unlikely. The father’s sister assumed care

                
Page 1 asserts that none of the close relatives wanted to assume care, yet this passage identifies Pál’s sister as having done so.
