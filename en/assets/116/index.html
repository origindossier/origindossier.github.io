Bojana Vujić’s panel grants 30 days for a written position and fee-exemption evidence — 5 November 2024

Hearing record P2.1435/24 — Bojana Vujić’s panel, 5 November 2024

Complete translation of both pages of the court record supplied by the author. The court text is followed by a separate editorial source note and the author’s later account concerning the summons and the end of his representation.

The record is dated 5 November 2024. It records a non-public hearing from 11:00 to 11:30, with the plaintiff present in person. The Appellate Court ruling R.22/24 of 21 August 2024 is handed to him at this hearing.

Source page 2 — Full translation

It is recorded that the defendant’s authorized representative has not appeared at the hearing, having been duly summoned by the ruling dated 16 September 2024.

It is recorded that the Appellate Court in Novi Sad’s ruling R.22/24 of 21 August 2024 is handed directly to the plaintiff.

The plaintiff asks the court, given that he is a party unversed in law and wishes to seek legal assistance, to grant him a period of 30 days within which he will state his position in writing on the further course of proceedings in this legal matter.

He particularly emphasizes that, in the meantime, he has lost his job; that he will submit proof of this, in the form of an agreed termination of his employment contract; and that he asks the court to exempt him from paying court fees after that proof has been submitted.

He proposes that no hearing in the case be scheduled until his submission has been received.

The panel makes the following

RULING

The court will decide the further course of proceedings in writing, after the plaintiff’s written submission has been delivered.

The plaintiff is granted the requested period of 30 days from the day this ruling is announced to state his position in writing on the further course of proceedings in this legal matter and to submit evidence concerning exemption from court fees.

Deliver a copy of this record to the Centre for Social Work of the City of Novi Sad.

Completed at 11:30.

Court clerk — parties — presiding judge

[Printed page number: 2.]

Editorial source note

The ruling grants a 30-day period for a written submission and evidence concerning court-fee exemption; it does not itself grant that exemption. The printed instructions on page 1 concerning small claims and Article 118 are form text, not a new court decision classifying this claim as a small-claims case. The duly summoned representative described in the court text belongs to the defendant CSR.

Later author’s account — the second summons for the same hearing

The author explains that he was told that the summons for the same hearing was being sent for the second time. He does not know whether the first summons was sent to him or to his former lawyer, who had informed the court that she no longer represented him. He received the latest summons and came to the hearing alone. This is his later account; it is separate from the court record’s statement about the defendant CSR’s representative being duly summoned.

This establishes his attendance in this procedural stage; his later explanation of arriving alone after representation ended is preserved separately.

ZPP — Article 118 · Reading and correcting the hearing record

                
The form’s printed reference to “particulars under Article 118(1)” does not match the current Act’s numbering. Those particulars are in Article 116(1), to which the current small-claims provision, Article 474, expressly refers. The printed numbering corresponds to Articles 118(1) and 473 of the 2004 Act. This is evidence of an outdated reference in the form; it does not establish that the court applied the former Act or classified this identity/adoption dispute as a small claim.

Legal assistance sought after loss of employment
