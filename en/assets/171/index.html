BASIC COURT IN NOVI SAD
Case no.: P2.1435/24

PLAINTIFF: Milan Petrović (born Milivoj Miletić), [REDACTED], Kragujevac

DEFENDANT: Centre for Social Work of the City of Novi Sad, Zmaj Ognjena Vuka 13, Novi Sad (hereinafter: the defendant)

PLAINTIFF’S SUBMISSION
(supplementing evidentiary proposals, regularising procedural prerequisites and time limits)

Dear Court,

Following the minutes of the hearing of 15 October 2025 and the order that I state my further evidentiary proposals in writing within 15 days, I submit the following.

I. PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK AND PURPOSE

The case is formally classified as P2 (annulment of the full adoption of 1979), but its factual and legal foundations extend beyond a typical family-law dispute: it concerns protection of the right to identity and the consequences of administrative assimilation, together with actions by the guardianship authority without a legal basis and outside the time limits and prerequisites then in force. I propose that the Court examine the complete case records and ensure proper representation of the defendant, so that its decision rests on a complete and lawful factual foundation.

II. EVIDENTIARY PROPOSALS AND RECORDS TO BE OBTAINED OFFICIALLY

1. Higher/Municipal Court in Novi Sad — divorce file P-1642/76 (possibly an archival reference)

— the complete file, particularly: the statement of Pavle Lakatoš (acknowledgment of extramarital paternity and identification of the mother as “Cvita”, together with his declaration that he is Hungarian); the statements of Vera/Veronika Lakatoš and witness Roža Kopljan (the statement that the family knew of the child’s existence).

Relevance: refutes the claim “father unknown”; confirms the plaintiff’s identity and paternal (Hungarian) origin.

2. Municipal Court in Novi Sad — probate file O.196/79 (estate of the late Pavle Lakatoš)

— the minutes of 19 March 1979; the Court’s letter/conclusion of 20 July 1979 addressed to the guardianship authority (order to appoint a guardian to protect the child’s inheritance rights).

Relevance: confirms that the defendant had no basis for establishing full adoption on 21 June 1979; the judicial order was issued only on 20 July 1979, and concerned protection of inheritance rights, not adoption.

3. Civil registry service in Novi Sad

— an extract from the marriage register (1962) for the marriage of Vasić Milorad and Tomić Gospava (ethnicity: Serbian man/Serbian woman).

Relevance: confirms the plaintiff’s maternal (Croatian) and paternal (Hungarian) origin; confirms that the Vasić adopters are of Serbian ethnicity (relevant to the pattern of assimilation).

4. Centre for Social Work, Novi Sad — complete file on the full adoption (21 June 1979)

— adoption minutes, opinions of the professional service (social worker, educational specialist, psychologist), medical certificates, summonses, proofs of service, instructions to the registrar, all acts concerning registration/cancellation; the complete set of “preceding” acts (how the child came within the Centre’s competence; communication with the probate court).

Relevance: examination of lawfulness, competence and time limits (Articles 2, 5, 10, 14, 15, 30, 31 and 41 of the SAP Vojvodina Adoption Act, 1976), possible conflict of roles (guardian/adopter), formulaic and contradictory assessments.

5. Centre for Social Work, Novi Sad — minutes of 12 September 1984 (Milorad Vasić’s statement giving “consent to the adoption of the minor Vasić Milan”)

— the complete documentation and accompanying acts.

Relevance: the legal paradox of a “second adoption” after full adoption had already been established in 1979; examine the legal validity of such “consent”.

Note on the location of the files (for the Court’s guidance): divorce file P-1642/76 is held in the archives of this Court, while probate file O.196/79 is held in the Archives of the City of Novi Sad.

III. REGULARISING PROCEDURAL PREREQUISITES (AUTHORITY TO REPRESENT AND TIME LIMITS)

1. Authority of the defendant’s representative (Branislav Čolak).

Order the defendant to submit, no later than the hearing on 26 November 2025, the original or a certified copy of the written authority to represent it and evidence of the representative’s status (whether he is employed by the defendant or is an external person). Since the minutes state “whose authority to represent is being submitted to the file”, I ask the Court to record the exact date on which that authority was received and establish that, until that date, the defendant had not proved that it was properly represented. If it is not submitted: consider the defendant improperly represented and the actions of a person without valid authority to be without legal effect. This ensures lawful representation of the defendant and prevents a possible reopening of proceedings because of procedural irregularities.

2. Submission of the complete documentation.

Order the defendant to submit by the hearing on 26 November 2025: (i) the complete full-adoption file (1979), (ii) the acts showing how it acquired competence before 21 June 1979, (iii) the Centre’s minutes of 12 September 1984 and accompanying acts concerning “consent”, (iv) the complete simple-adoption file (1984). In the event of non-compliance: draw adverse evidentiary conclusions concerning the disputed issues (competence, fulfilment of the conditions, course of the procedure).

IV. CONCISE LEGAL BASIS (FAMILY-LAW ASPECT — 1976)

— Articles 2, 5, 10, 14, 15, 30, 31 and 41 of the SAP Vojvodina Adoption Act (1976): full adoption may be established only if the prerequisites are fulfilled (no later than the age of five, and, if the parents are known and alive, with their consent), following a preparatory procedure and exclusively in the child’s interests.

— The full-adoption decision is dated 21 June 1979, seven days before my fifth birthday and before the judicial order of 20 July 1979 (which ordered protection of inheritance rights, not adoption).

— The father acknowledged paternity in litigation, and the mother was alive and known; the defendant’s claims “father unknown/address unknown” are refuted by the divorce and probate files and civil registry documents.

— “Second adoption” (1984): this legal institution is not provided for after full adoption has already been established; any changes in status must proceed exclusively through annulment/setting aside, not through a new adoption. The adopter’s “consent” of 1984 is a legal anomaly and confirms a continuity of unlawful conduct.

IVA. CLAUSE ON THE APPLICATION OF LAW (LEX TEMPORIS AND PRESENT-DAY ACTIONS)

— The actions of 1979/1984 are assessed exclusively under the law in force at the time they were undertaken: the Constitution of the SFRY (1974) — Articles 199, 214 and 245–247; ICCPR (in force for the SFRY from 23 March 1976) — Article 27 (with the interpretation of the HRC, General Comment no. 23); ICERD (in force from 4 January 1969); and the SAP Vojvodina Adoption Act (1976).

— Subsequent legislation and international standards (Constitution of the Republic of Serbia 2006, minority laws, Anti-Discrimination Act, FCNM, ECHR, ECRML, CRC, etc.) do not apply retroactively

IVB. CHRONOLOGY IN DOMESTIC LAW AFTER 1979 (REINFORCEMENT, NOT RETROACTIVE APPLICATION)

— FRY (1992–2003): Constitution of the FRY; Act on the Protection of the Rights and Freedoms of National Minorities (Official Gazette of the FRY 11/2002) — language/script, personal names according to the minority’s orthography, official use of language.

— Republic of Serbia (from 2006): Constitution of the Republic of Serbia 2006 — Article 78 (prohibition of forced assimilation), Article 79 (special minority rights); Act on the Protection of the Rights and Freedoms of National Minorities; Act on the Official Use of Languages and Scripts (1991; amendments 2010/2018); Anti-Discrimination Act (2009; amendments 2021).

IVC. INTERNATIONAL INSTRUMENTS (TODAY — INTERPRETATIVE AND FOR CURRENT ACTIONS)

— ECHR (for Serbia from 2004) — Articles 8 + 14; Protocol 12;

— FCNM (in force 2001) — Article 5(2) (prohibition of assimilation against one’s will) and rights to language/name/education (Articles 10–14, 11);

— ECRML (in force 2006) — Parts II/III;

— CRC — Article 8 (the child’s right to identity) and Article 30 (rights of a child belonging to a minority).

Note: these standards serve the Court as reinforcement and interpretative guidance, and apply to the defendant’s present-day conduct (failure to submit files, denial of the violation, maintenance of an unlawful situation).

V. FACTUAL NOTE: IDENTITY AND ASSIMILATION

— KJ-8/the birth register confirm that the mother is Croatian; divorce file P-1642/76 confirms paternity and Hungarian paternal origin; the marriage register (1962) — the adopters are a Serbian man/Serbian woman.

— In practice, the child’s personal name was changed and original identity suppressed in favour of the adopters’ identity — a pattern of administrative assimilation incompatible with the Constitution of 1974, ICCPR/ICERD (then), as well as the Constitution of the Republic of Serbia 2006 and minority laws (today).

— The adopter’s “consent” of 1984 that “the child should go for adoption” confirms conduct outside the law and the logic of full adoption.

VI. DISCREET NOTE ON THE PUBLIC-LAW DIMENSION

Given the nature of the violations (identity, language, belonging, inheritance rights) and the continuity of the guardianship authority’s conduct, the case carries broader significance in public law. Timely obtaining of the complete documentation and proper representation of the defendant are the best guarantee that the decision will be legally correct and able to stand on its own.

VII. PROCEDURAL SIGNIFICANCE OF THE DEFENDANT’S POSITION IN 2025 (CONTINUITY OF THE VIOLATION)

In 2025, in its response to the settlement proposal and in submissions, the defendant stated that no laws had been breached and that everything was “in accordance with the legislation then in force”. It thereby:

(a) accepted discussion of the merits — the actions of 1979/1984 are assessed under the law then in force (Constitution of the SFRY 1974; ICCPR/ICERD; SAP Vojvodina Adoption Act 1976); and

(b) assumed responsibility for present-day procedural actions and omissions, which are assessed under the current law of the Republic of Serbia and contemporary international standards (Constitution of the Republic of Serbia 2006; minority laws; Anti-Discrimination Act; FCNM; ECHR).

Thus, there is a continuity of the violation, and present-day maintenance of an unlawful situation falls within the scope of current law.

VIII. PROPOSALS TO THE COURT

  1. Grant the above proposals for obtaining records officially and order the defendant to submit the complete documentation (points II/4 and II/5) by 26 November 2025; in the event of non-compliance, draw adverse evidentiary conclusions.
  2. Order the defendant to submit by 26 November 2025 the written authority to represent it and evidence of the status of representative Branislav Čolak; record the date on which that authority was received. In its absence, consider the defendant improperly represented and the actions of a person without valid authority to be without legal effect.
  3. Establish a standard for future adjournments: every request because of illness must be supported by a credible medical certificate (institution/stamp, signature, date and time of examination, period of incapacity); otherwise, hold the hearings.
  4. When assessing the facts, apply the law in force at the time of the actions (Constitution of the SFRY 1974 — Articles 199, 214 and 245–247; ICCPR — Article 27; ICERD — Articles 2 and 5; SAP Vojvodina Adoption Act 1976), and assess the defendant’s present-day conduct in the light of the Constitution of the Republic of Serbia 2006 (Articles 78–79), the Act on the Protection of the Rights and Freedoms of National Minorities, the Act on the Official Use of Languages and Scripts, the Anti-Discrimination Act, FCNM and ECHR.
  5. After the decision becomes final, enable the initiation of the appropriate corrections to civil registers in accordance with the established facts concerning identity and origin.

  2. Once the obstacle created by full adoption has been removed, I reserve the right to initiate proceedings to establish paternity and the accompanying entries/corrections.

Editorial legal analysis — representation, the hearing record and written continuation

Prepared with AI assistance from the supplied documents and the ZPP applicable in October 2025. This analysis is separate from the plaintiff’s submission and the court’s findings.

Branislav Čolak’s status requires a concrete check. The 15 October attendance entry calls him a CSR law graduate whose power of attorney is being submitted to the file. That formula does not supply its receipt date, employment evidence or proof of the judicial examination. Article 85(4) permits a legal entity to act through an attorney or its employee who is a law graduate with the judicial examination passed. These statutory alternatives make the plaintiff’s request for status evidence concrete; an online namesake does not establish who represented CSR or that person’s status on the hearing date. ZPP, Article 85(4), PDF page 16.

Authority and later approval must both be examined. Articles 90 and 91 require written authority at the first procedural action and continuing judicial control; Article 91 also expressly recognises the party’s subsequent approval of unauthorised actions. The proper inquiry is therefore the actual instrument, filing date, scope and any subsequent approval, rather than treating every action as automatically ineffective from the attendance wording alone. In Rev 2731/2022, the Supreme Court explained that general litigation authority is not invariably subject to certification and addressed approval of actions. That judgment concerns a different dispute, not Čolak’s actual authority. ZPP, Articles 90–91, PDF page 18; Rev 2731/2022, 31 March 2022.

The record must preserve what the plaintiff actually stated. Article 118 gives parties the right to inspect or read the record, object to its contents, and have corrections or additions entered at the end; unaccepted objections are entered on request. The eight-day objection in Article 121(3) specifically concerns a material discrepancy between an audio recording and its written transcript. It must not be mechanically applied to every ordinary written record. Here “no objections to what was read” appears before the plaintiff’s examination and refers to the previously read case file. It is not a blanket acceptance of the later wording of his testimony. ZPP, Article 118, PDF page 23; Article 121, PDF page 24.

The written statement and the later submission have distinct procedural roles. The 15 October record expressly enters the written statement and records service of a copy on CSR, then grants fifteen days from announcement of the order for further evidentiary proposals. Under Article 103, the first counted day is the following day; that ordinary calendar calculation ends on 30 October. The present text is dated 27 October. Article 104 treats the actual registered-post submission date as the court-submission date, but the closing notation is not a postal receipt. The materials therefore establish the content and written date while leaving the actual dispatch/receipt record to be checked. ZPP, Articles 103–104, PDF pages 20–21.

The medical-evidence requirements remain the plaintiff’s proposals. The detailed certificate, issuance-log and preservation demands in the 2 October submission, reaffirmed on 27 October, seek verifiable justification and dispatch information. They are not evidence that the court imposed seven-day or three-day requirements, or a universally prescribed statutory medical form. The concrete questions remain what justified the earlier absence, whether substitute representation was feasible, and when notice was dispatched and received. The separately archived cancellation and postal evidence permit those questions to be tested. Earlier documents and verified legal analysis; 2 October submission.
