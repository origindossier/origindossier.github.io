Establishment of the subject-matter and territorial jurisdiction of the Higher Court in Novi Sad

The right to bring this claim for annulment of full adoption before the Higher Court as the court with subject-matter jurisdiction is prescribed by Article 42 in conjunction with Article 28 of the Law on Adoption of Vojvodina (“Official Gazette of SAP Vojvodina”, No. 24/76), which was in force when the adoption was established. We base the application of that law in this case on Article 358, paragraph 1 of the currently applicable Family Law (“Official Gazette of the Republic of Serbia”, Nos. 18/2005, 72/2011 — other law, and 6/2015), which provides:

“An adoption established before the commencement of the application of this law shall be governed by the regulations that were in force until the date on which the application of this law commenced.”

Article 28 of the Law on Adoption of SAP Vojvodina (“Official Gazette of SAP Vojvodina”, No. 24/76) provides:

“The district court shall decide on the annulment of adoption. The parents, the adopter, the public prosecutor and the adoptee after acquiring legal capacity shall have the right to bring an action for annulment of adoption.”

As district courts no longer exist following the definition of the new court network under the Law on the Organisation of Courts (“Official Gazette of the Republic of Serbia”, Nos. 116/08, 104/09..88/18 and 10/23), and the subject-matter jurisdiction formerly exercised by district courts is now exercised by higher courts, we therefore address this statement of claim to the Higher Court.

We base the territorial jurisdiction of the Higher Court in Novi Sad on the fact that the defendant guardianship authority is located within its territory. That authority issued the decision on the plaintiff's full adoption while committing enormous failures in establishing the statutory conditions for that adoption to take place. Those conditions were not met, rendering the adoption absolutely null and void, as will be explained in the further narrative of the statement of claim. All of this caused irreparable harm to the life of the then minor adoptee, Miletić Milivoj (as the plaintiff was then named), rather than serving his best interests in life.

Facts of the case

Although we do not wish to burden this statement of claim with unnecessary narrative, we must nevertheless emphasise that it is impossible to view this entire case properly without acquainting the Court with certain facts that preceded the adoption proceedings themselves. We will therefore summarise them

in the following passages so that the Court can gain a comprehensive picture and understand the extent of the illegality committed in this case.

The plaintiff was born on 28 June 1974 in Novi Sad as Miletić Milivoj, from an extramarital relationship. His biological mother, now deceased, was named Miletić Cvita, and his father, also deceased, was Pavle Lakatoš. Although, from a legal standpoint, the father of the minor Miletić Milivoj had not been established because his particulars as the child's father were not entered in the birth register, he was de facto known as the plaintiff's biological father to a wider circle of people, primarily the closest members of his family: his wife Veronika Terezija Lakatoš and the children Roža, Iren and Ištvan. We point out to the Court that we understand that this is not a paternity action, but it is impossible to view the whole matter of the unlawful adoption properly without saying a few words about the facts relating to the plaintiff's origins.

The following primarily supports the assertion that the late Pavle Lakatoš was known to be the plaintiff's biological father. The late Pavle Lakatoš divorced his wife Veronika because of disagreements in their married life. In their divorce proceedings before the District Court in Novi Sad, case No. P.1642/76, the records of the hearings contain statements in several places, made by Pavle Lakatoš himself as a party to the proceedings, by his wife Veronika as the other party, by their daughter Roža, who wished to testify in her parents' divorce proceedings and in whose care the then minor plaintiff was even placed, and by the witness Gabrić Nikola. Those statements say that Pavle Lakatoš had fathered an extramarital child — a son — with the day labourer Cvita, who helped him run his farm and with whom he had meanwhile begun living together.

That fact was plainly disputed by no one. Indeed, the plaintiff's half-sister Roža cared for her little half-brother until Pavle Lakatoš died on 7 November 1978 and the question arose of inheriting his estate, which was not without value.

None of this might in itself have been strange were it not for the fact that it all happened after it was plainly known that, if expert examination established that the minor Milivoj was the deceased's son, he would definitely be entitled to part of the inheritance, regardless of his being a child from an extramarital relationship. To prevent that, there was, unfortunately, a legal route that someone evidently knew very well: the child was to be immediately placed for full adoption. Incidentally, full adoption was at that time more the exception than the rule because the institution of full adoption had only just been introduced into the legal system. This is because Article 38 of the Law on Adoption of SAP Vojvodina (“Official Gazette of SAP Vojvodina”, No. 24/76), which was then in force, states:

“The establishment of paternity and maternity of a fully adopted child is not permitted.”

The matter is so obvious that it could not be more obvious.

“The minor Milivoj Miletić is currently in the care of his grandmother and grandfather, the deceased's parents.”

The period within which the defendant supposedly carried out all checks of the adopters' suitability is unprecedented in a matter as extremely sensitive as the adoption of children. It is generally known that adoption proceedings take years and that many prospective adopters even give up because of the complicated and complex procedure. This, of course, resulted in the then minor Miletić Milivoj being placed for adoption with persons who were completely unsuitable to be adopters, because the checks of those persons' suitability were not carried out properly — if any checks were carried out at all — given that the defendant's reports on the adopters' suitability contain a series of platitudes repeated over and over in all similar reports and situations. Anything can be put on paper. In practical terms, it was impossible to assess the adopters' personalities, their relationship with each other, their living circumstances and similar matters adequately, reliably and responsibly within such a short period.

One might again ask why everything was carried out at such speed. The answer is very clear when the following is taken into account: the minor Milivoj was approaching his fifth birthday. He would turn five on 28 June 1979, after which, under the previously mentioned Law on Adoption of SAP Vojvodina, it would no longer have been possible to establish full adoption, because only a child who had not yet reached the age of five could be fully adopted, subject to the fulfilment of the other cumulative conditions. Thus, it was necessary to rush through the procedure before the adoptee turned five. We have already explained that full adoption removes the possibility of establishing paternity and therefore of claiming an inheritance.

Through its employees, using all manner of platitudes and paeans of praise about the adopters, the defendant established completely untrue facts about their suitability in the reports prepared by social worker Vera Radovanović on 12 June 1979, pedagogue Miroslava Gudelj on 18 June 1979, and psychologist Cvetko Ratković on 19 June 1979. To this day, the plaintiff has not been given access to the medical report on the adopters' suitability.

It must also be stressed and underlined that social worker Vera Radovanović was the minor adoptee's “ad hoc guardian”, meaning that she was supposed to protect his rights and interests impartially in the adoption proceedings, while the same person was preparing the findings and opinion on the suitability of

A series of facts demonstrates the untruth of the assertions that the late Milorad and Gospava Vasić were suitable to be the plaintiff's adopters and to raise him to become a useful member of the socialist society of that time, as required by Article 11, paragraph 1, item c) of the Law on Adoption of SAP Vojvodina, failing which the adoption is null and void.

The plaintiff's late adopters, Milorad and Gospava Vasić, were a completely dysfunctional married couple with serious, long-standing problems in their marital relationship, dating back many years before they adopted the plaintiff. Milorad was a very disagreeable and difficult man and frequently practised marital infidelity. He was nine years younger than his wife and in the prime of his life, which demanded satisfaction of a sexual drive that was not pronounced in his wife; this, among other things, caused problems in their marriage.

As is known to the witness Vinka Ignjatić of Budisava, who was their neighbour and knew the adopters well, Gospava complained to her that Milorad mistreated her and that every little thing bothered him. Were these truly suitable adopters, who should have been like a biological father and mother to the child, given him all their love and tenderness and raised him in a healthy spirit so that the child would have a chance of a successful life? We must view the entire situation from the perspective of socialist society and its values, because the Law on Adoption requires this. The highest aim of socialism, at least ideologically, was “the personal happiness of the human being”.

Instead of fulfilling its task and finding and securing adopters who would give the child the best conditions for proper growth, development and happiness, in which the child would develop so as one day to become a “useful member of socialist society”, the defendant placed him in the worst possible living conditions. He escaped those conditions solely and exclusively because of the circumstances of life that took him out of that “caring environment”: adopter Milorad left others to care for him, and adopter Gospava departed this world.

None of this would have happened if proper checks had been made as to what kind of people the adopters were. The child's life would not have been made a literal hell, the consequences of which can never be remedied. The plaintiff never experienced these imposed adopters as real adopters or parents and therefore never claimed anything after their deaths, although he is their heir by law.

Legal framework

Article 41, in conjunction with Article 11 of the Law on Adoption of SAP Vojvodina (“Official Gazette of SAP Vojvodina”, No. 24/76), prescribes the conditions for nullity of adoption — not cumulative, but alternative. Thus, it provides that:

“A person who may be considered unlikely to raise the adoptee to become a useful member of socialist society is not permitted to adopt.”

That was definitely the case here, not just for one adopter but for both. This will also be established in the evidentiary proceedings and constitutes sufficient grounds for annulling this full adoption.

The aforementioned article also states:

“A person suffering from an illness that may endanger the adoptee's health and life is not permitted to adopt.”

We have well-founded suspicions that Gospava Vasić was not an entirely healthy woman, and we have proposed that the Court officially check that circumstance with the competent medical institution.

Likewise, Article [number absent in the source] of the Law on Adoption of SAP Vojvodina (“Official Gazette of SAP Vojvodina”, No. 24/76) provides:

“Only a child who has not yet reached the age of five, whose parents are not alive or are unknown, or have abandoned the child and whose whereabouts have not been established even after the expiry of a period of six months from the abandonment of the child, may be fully adopted.”

The adoptee's mother was not unknown, her whereabouts were not unknown, and she was alive when the adoption was established. The plaintiff therefore does not understand why

her consent to his adoption was not sought, even though her residential address was known. There are therefore several statutory grounds on which this adoption is null and void, and the existence of only one of them is certainly sufficient for the adoption to be annulled.

Prayer for relief

In view of all the relevant facts set out above and the stated legal framework, we consider that the conditions for annulment of the plaintiff's full adoption have been met. We therefore request that, after an oral hearing and the taking of all evidence, the above Court deliver the following:

JUDGMENT — PROPOSED IN THE STATEMENT OF CLAIM

The plaintiff's claim IS UPHELD as well-founded.

The full adoption established and declared by the decision of the Self-Managing Community of Interest for Social Welfare of Novi Sad, No. UP.03-507/79 of 21 June 1979 in Novi Sad, IS ANNULLED as absolutely null and void. By that decision, Vasić Milorad, born on 15 February 1941 in Osječani, Municipality of Doboj, and Vasić Gospava, born on 21 August 1932 in D. Klakar, Municipality of Doboj, both residing in Budisava, Petefi Šandora Street No. 52, adopted the minor Miletić Milivoj, and the adopted minor Miletić Milivoje received the new name Vasić Milan.

The defendant IS ORDERED, immediately upon receiving this judgment, to issue a decision annulling the Decision of the Self-Managing Community of Interest for Social Welfare of Novi Sad, No. UP.03-507/79 of 21 June 1979, which approved the subsequent entry in the birth register of the particulars of the adopters Vasić Milorad and Vasić Gospava of Budisava as the parents of the fully adopted child, the plaintiff in these proceedings, so that annulment of that decision shall restore the effect of the first registration of the birth of the fully adopted child, the plaintiff in these proceedings.

The defendant IS ORDERED to reimburse the plaintiff in full for the costs of these civil proceedings, in the amount in which they are incurred, together with statutory default interest from the date of enforceability until payment, all within 15 days from the date on which the judgment becomes final, failing which enforcement shall follow.
