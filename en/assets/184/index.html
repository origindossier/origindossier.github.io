Editorial legal note: the 1984 records and evidential relevance

This is an editorial legal analysis prepared with AI, separate from the submission and any court ruling.

CSR states that it supplies the records concerning the 1984 incomplete adoption but regards them as irrelevant because the plaintiff does not seek annulment of that adoption. This reasoning skips which fact the records may establish about the earlier full adoption and subsequent conduct. The author specifically connects Milorad’s 1984 consent record to the legal paradox of another adoption after the 1979 full adoption; proposing that evidence does not automatically change the relief sought.

Article 3(1) of the Serbian Civil Procedure Act confines the court’s decision to the claims made. Article 228 requires parties to present facts and propose evidence supporting their claim or challenging their opponent’s allegations. Article 229 covers all facts important to the decision and leaves the court to choose the evidence establishing material facts. The limits of the claim therefore do not automatically exclude evidence concerning a later act: its connection to a particular disputed fact must be assessed. Official Civil Procedure Act, Articles 3, 228 and 229

Not disputing the content and authenticity of Milorad’s statement is separate from the legal validity of that consent. That distinction must be examined in relation to the author’s argument about the void initial procedure and the subsequent identity construct.

ZPP — Article 228 · Facts and evidence supporting or contesting a claim

                
The editorial analysis invokes the parties’ duty to present facts and propose evidence when explaining why the claimant may rely on the 1984 records to challenge CSR’s account of the earlier full adoption.
