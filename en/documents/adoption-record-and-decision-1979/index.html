1979 full adoption record and decision - operative identity overwrite

The operative 21 June 1979 record by which minor Miletić Milivoj was fully adopted by Vasić Milorad and Gospava, renamed Vasić Milan, and routed to the registrar for entry of the adopters as parents. Read against the 1974 birth entry and P-1642/76, this is the administrative mechanism that overwrote the Croatian maternal and Hungarian paternal origin with the Vasić adoptive frame.

Questions raised by this document

Article 34: parental data in the full-adoption record

Article 34 prohibits entering information about the adoptee’s parents in the record establishing full adoption. This record nevertheless describes the mother’s alleged unknown whereabouts and failure to perform parental duties as the basis for guardian consent. Article 33 only says that prior parental consent is not read aloud at the hearing; it does not remove the separate requirement in Article 30(4). This is the direct record-content conflict raised in the revision; Article 34 alone is not a listed ground of nullity under Article 41.

Revision of 29 May 2026: the appellate judgment confronted with the 1976 law and the identity evidence
Why this source is linked
Related source

The 1979 record Up.03-507/79 is the act challenged. The revision compares its parental information and consent narrative with Article 34 and the separate full-adoption conditions.

“Full adoption may not be dissolved.”

In the factual part of its judgment, the Court of Appeal accepts that:

  • on 21 June 1979 the plaintiff was fully adopted by Milorad and Gospava Vasić;
  • on 26 September 1984 the plaintiff was adopted again by Periša and Georgina Petrović.

If the first full adoption was lawful and indissoluble, a second adoption of the same child could not lawfully have been carried out.

If the second adoption was nevertheless carried out, that cannot have no bearing on assessment of the legal sustainability of the first full adoption.

The second-instance court refused to draw any conclusion from this direct statutory contradiction, reasoning that the validity of the second adoption is not the subject of the proceedings.

That approach is wrong.

The subject of the proceedings is the nullity of the 1979 full adoption.

The fact that the same system placed the same child for adoption again in 1984 is directly relevant to assessing whether the 1979 act was indeed lawfully, fully and permanently established.

The Court of Appeal neutralised the legal significance of this fact instead of assessing it.

It thereby failed to apply Article 40 and assess the status consequences under Articles 35, 37 and 38 SAPVAA.

7. The second-instance court incorrectly applied Article 34 of the Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina

Article 34 SAPVAA provides:

“Information about the adoptee’s parents shall not be entered in the record establishing full adoption.”

Yet the Court of Appeal states in its reasoning that the full-adoption record Up.03-507/79 of 21 June 1979 records:

  • that the minor Milivoj’s mother has been at an unknown place of residence for a prolonged period;
  • that she does not perform her parental duties without justifiable reasons;
  • that Vera Radovanović was appointed ad hoc guardian and consents to adoption.

In other words, according to the Court of Appeal’s own factual description, the full-adoption record contains information about the adoptee’s parent, and uses it as the central factual basis for establishing full adoption.

That directly contradicts Article 34 SAPVAA.

The Court of Appeal did not assess this legal consequence at all.

Although this violation was not separately numbered in the appeal, it follows from the contested judgment’s own factual description and is a legal consequence of the facts the second-instance court itself accepted as established.

It is therefore directly relevant in proceedings on this appeal on points of law, as further confirmation that the lower courts applied SAPVAA selectively.

V. INCORRECT APPLICATION OF SUBSTANTIVE LAW

1. Incorrect application of Article 30 of the SAP Vojvodina Law on Adoption

The first-instance court formally cited Article 30 but did not apply it specifically to the established facts.

The court did not clarify whether it brought the case within paragraph 1 or paragraph 4 of that article.

If the case falls within paragraph 1, the court had to establish whether the parents were unknown, or whether the child had been abandoned and the parents’ whereabouts remained unestablished even after the six-month period had elapsed.

If the case falls within paragraph 4, the court had to clarify who was unknown or deceased, who the other parent was, and where that other parent’s prior consent was, or the legal basis on which it had been lawfully replaced.

None of this was done.

2. Incorrect application of Article 41 of the SAP Vojvodina Law on Adoption

The court reduced Article 41 to a declaration that no grounds for nullity existed, without genuinely checking whether the specific requirements of Articles 30 and 31 were met.

It thereby effectively left the guardianship body to act both as the creator of the contested act and as the measure of its own lawfulness, accepting that the body had already “established” this.

3. Disregard of Article 40 and the logic of Article 7

By mentioning the second adoption in 1984 without any legal explanation, the court disregarded the logic of the law then in force.
