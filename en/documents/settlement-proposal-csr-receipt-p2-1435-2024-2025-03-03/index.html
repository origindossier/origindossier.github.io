Settlement proposal: nullity, paternity and original identity — CSR receipt confirmed on 3 March 2025

The author sends a detailed settlement proposal after the February hearing: recognition of the nullity of the 1979 adoption, restoration of original identity and continuation of paternity proceedings. He sets out his genetic and family origins, warns against continued violent assimilation and asks for a reply within seven days. CSR’s reception office confirms email receipt at 08:30 CET.

Novi Sad Social Work Centre 2025-03-03
The original email was sent on 3 March 2025 at 07:01:52 UTC, equivalent to 08:01:52 CET, forwarded at 08:15 CET and acknowledged by the CSR reception office at 08:30 CET. Its three pages preserve the complete proposal and its consequences, not an accepted settlement. The author proposes that the Centre recognize the nullity of his illegal adoption of 1979 and withdraw its opposition, allowing him to pursue establishment of paternity before the competent court in Kragujevac. He invokes his original Croatian maternal and Hungarian paternal roots, documents and DNA matches, the search for his mother and the timing seven days before his fifth birthday. His source expressly says “incomplete adoption” in that birthday argument; the full translation retains that word. His undertaking to end legal and other contact, with no further obligations for the Centre, is conditional on acceptance of his proposal. He describes continued opposition as violent assimilation and warns of responsibility extending to the current management and employees, international proceedings, media exposure and individual legal consequences. He requests a substantive email reply within seven days and confirmation of receipt of the message and attachments within 24 hours. These are his requested periods. The email names two attachments, including the genetic and historical origin analysis. The office’s reply states only that the email was duly received. A separately marked later author’s clarification records his good faith, offer of peaceful resolution, wish to establish paternity and return to his original roots, and his conclusion that institutions abused his father’s death and continue forced Serbianization. No later refusal is invented.

IX. ATTEMPTED SETTLEMENT IN GOOD FAITH AND DISREGARD OF THE WARNING ABOUT PERSONAL RESPONSIBILITY

As early as the beginning of 2025, before this phase of the proceedings, the plaintiff offered the Centre for Social Work of the City of Novi Sad an opportunity to emerge from this case honourably and professionally through a peaceful resolution. The defendant thereby had a clear opportunity:

  • to acknowledge the unlawfulness of the full adoption in 1979;
  • to enable restoration of the child’s true identity;
  • to avoid international escalation and the issue of the personal responsibility of present managers and officials.

Instead, the Centre chose to ignore that opportunity.

1. Chronology: email “Settlement proposal — P2 1435/24” (3 March 2025)

On 3 March 2025, the plaintiff sent an email to the Centre’s official address, novisad.csr@minrzs.gov.rs, with the subject:

“Subject: Settlement proposal — P2 1435/24”.

At 08:30 that same day, the Centre’s reception office replied:

“Your email has been duly received. Kind regards. Reception Office.”

(Note: the original text is in Cyrillic.)

Thus:

  • the Centre indisputably received the email;
  • it indisputably had access to the contents of the proposal;
  • it indisputably knew that a settlement was being offered.

After that acknowledgment of receipt, the Centre sent no substantive reply and showed no willingness to consider the settlement proposal.
