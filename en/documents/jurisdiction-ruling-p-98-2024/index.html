Higher Court in Novi Sad P.98/2024 — Judge Dr Biljana Lepotić's ruling on jurisdiction, 26 February 2024

The court records the claim filed on 22 February 2024, declares that it lacks subject-matter jurisdiction and orders referral to the Basic Court after finality.

Higher Court in Novi Sad 2024-02-26
On 26 February 2024, Judge Dr Biljana Lepotić of the Higher Court in Novi Sad issued ruling P.98/2024 in Milan Petrović's claim against the Centre for Social Work of the City of Novi Sad for annulment of full adoption. The ruling expressly records that the claim was filed on 22 February 2024. The court declares that it lacks subject-matter jurisdiction and provides for the case file to be transferred to the Basic Court in Novi Sad after the ruling becomes final. This is a procedural ruling on jurisdiction, rather than a judgment deciding the annulment claim. The account of the relief sought includes annulment of the 1979 adoption and the subsequent birth-register entry, restoration of the first birth entry, costs, and an urgent hearing of Vinka Ignjatić under Article 284 of the Civil Procedure Law for preservation of evidence. The full translation preserves the source's citations to Article 24, paragraph 2 and its concluding citation to paragraph 1. A separate editorial transcript links the supplied court portal image and distinguishes receipt on 22 February, the action dates of 23 and 27 February, and the ruling and dispatch dated 26 February.

Article 15 · Initial jurisdiction review

Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Serbia

Biljana Lepotić invokes the court’s initial duty to examine jurisdiction from the claim and known facts when transferring P.98/2024 from the Higher Court. Article 15 concerns that jurisdictional assessment, not the validity of the 1979 adoption.

Short quotation from the source

Article 15 of the Civil Procedure Law provides that the court shall assess of its own motion whether it has jurisdiction and in what composition, on the basis of the allegations in the statement of claim and the facts known to the court. Article 17, paragraph 1 of that law provides that the court shall have regard of its own motion to its subject-matter jurisdiction throughout the proceedings.

Article 17 · Subject-matter jurisdiction

Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Serbia

The Higher Court invokes Article 17(1), its continuing duty to examine subject-matter jurisdiction, to transfer the case. The same article’s paragraph 4 excludes an appeal against this kind of transfer to a lower first-instance court of the same type; the ruling nevertheless gives a fifteen-day appeal instruction, later challenged in the 13 March submission.

Article 284 · Preservation of evidence

Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Serbia

The jurisdiction ruling records the claimant’s request to preserve Vinka Ignjatić’s testimony by an urgent hearing. Article 284 concerns evidence at risk of becoming unavailable or difficult to obtain. Reporting that request is not a decision granting it or proof that the witness was examined.

Short quotation from the source

The plaintiff also proposed that, pursuant to Article 284 of the Civil Procedure Law (ZPP), the Court in this legal matter grant the motion for the preservation of evidence and urgently schedule a hearing to examine the witness Ignjatić Vinka of Budisava, for the reasons set out in detail in the statement of claim.

Article 274 of the Family Law (“Official Gazette of the Republic of Serbia”, Nos. 18/2005, 72/2011 and 6/2015) provides that, in a dispute seeking annulment of adoption, territorial jurisdiction lies with the court in whose territory the guardianship authority before which the adoption was established is located.

Linking the original ruling allows comparison with the Appellate Court’s account and final jurisdictional decision.

By ruling P.98/2024 of 26 February 2024, the Higher Court in Novi Sad declared itself without subject-matter jurisdiction to proceed in this legal matter and, after the ruling became final, referred the case file to the Basic Court in Novi Sad, as the court with subject-matter and territorial jurisdiction, for further proceedings. It relied on Article 24(2) of the applicable Act on the Organization of Courts, in conjunction with Article 274 of the Family Act. In support of its decision, that court stated that the particular case was a family dispute brought for annulment of full adoption, to which Article 24(2) of the Act on the Organization of Courts must be applied, while Article 25 of the same Act prescribes the Higher Court’s jurisdiction in family disputes only in cases contesting or establishing paternity or maternity. The applicable Family Act, as a special law, does not prescribe the Higher Court’s subject-matter jurisdiction for this particular type of dispute, but only the court’s territorial jurisdiction.

CSR representative without power of attorney and jurisdiction objection — P2.470/24,24 April 2024
Why this source is linked
Related source

The 24 April record preserves counsel’s express criticism of Biljana Lepotić’s P.98/24 ruling of 26 February: she disputed the 15-day appeal instruction and the stamp stating finality on 20 March, invoking Article 17(4) of the Civil Procedure Act. These are counsel’s recorded statements; the original ruling provides the document being challenged.

We particularly emphasize that Higher Court judge Biljana Lepotić issued the ruling dated 26. 02. 2024, case number P.98/24, upon which she placed a stamp stating that the decision became final on 20. 03. 2024, although the instruction on legal remedy stated that an appeal against the ruling was permitted to the Court of Appeal in Novi Sad within 15 days of service, through this court. That instruction on legal remedy is wrong: under Article 17, paragraph 4, of the Civil Procedure Act, no appeal is permitted against a ruling by which the Higher Court declares itself to lack subject-matter jurisdiction to proceed further in this legal matter.
