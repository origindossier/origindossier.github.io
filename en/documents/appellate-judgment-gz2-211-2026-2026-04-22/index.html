Appellate Court dismisses the appeal: Branka Perc, Aleksandra Pekez Kalinić and Marko Prentović — Gž2 211/26, 22 April 2026

The panel of Branka Perc, Aleksandra Pekez Kalinić and Marko Prentović affirms dismissal of the claim to annul the 1979 full adoption. Its reasons cite Article 31 while describing Article 30, without selecting between paragraphs 1 and 4. The judgment is dated 22 April; the Basic Court receipt stamp is dated 30 April 2026.

Court of Appeal in Novi Sad 2026-04-22
In judgment Gž2 211/26, the Appellate Court in Novi Sad dismisses the appeal and affirms judgment P2 1435/2024 of 9 February 2026. The panel consists of presiding judge Branka Perc and members Aleksandra Pekez Kalinić and Marko Prentović. Full translations of all four pages and the searchable original are available. The court cites Article 31 of the 1976 Act, then describes Article 30's conditions concerning the child's age, parental status, the six-month period and the other parent's prior consent. It answers the objection that the applicable paragraph, 1 or 4, is unclear by stating that the conditions were in any event met. Local comments allow direct comparison with the parental and protective files, the original Act, the 1979 assessments and the grounds of appeal. The reasons find no inconsistencies in the professional assessments and treat the validity of the second adoption in 1984 as outside the subject of this case. The appeal, however, also uses the second act to test the legal effects of the first full adoption. Two separate costs statements are distinguished: that first-instance costs were not challenged by specific grounds, and that no claim for reimbursement of appellate costs was submitted. Thirty-five days separate the appeal dated 18 March from the decision of 22 April. This does not establish when the Appellate Court received the appeal. The stamp of 30 April confirms receipt at the Basic Court, not service on the appellant.

Questions raised by this document

Two distinct statutory conditions are not resolved by saying in any event

The appeal asks the court to select between Article 30 paragraph 1 and paragraph 4. For an abandoned child, the first requires at least six months without the parents' whereabouts being established; the second requires the other parent's prior consent where one parent is dead or unknown. The court says the conditions were in any event met but does not identify which branch it applies or which evidence satisfies its distinct condition. This is a concrete unresolved step in the reasons.

Revision of 29 May 2026: the appellate judgment confronted with the 1976 law and the identity evidence
Why this source is linked
Related source

The revision directly challenges Gž2 211/26. Page 13 identifies the appellate attribution of Article 30 conditions to Article 31; pages 5–7 challenge “in any event” and the absence of a specified consent/search basis.

No such consent exists. The second-instance court attempts to fill that gap by stating that the mother “could not have been available to give consent”, while the ad hoc guardian Vera Radovanović consented to adoption. Under the full-adoption regime, however, it is not enough to establish that somebody else signed a declaration. It must be established that the law permits such substitution. Article 42 SAPVAA, governing which provisions of the general adoption regime also apply, as appropriate, to full adoption, does not refer to Article 5, which concerns a guardian’s consent in place of a parent’s in certain situations under the general regime. This means that Article 30(4) remains a specific, strict rule for full adoption. Where one parent is dead or unknown, the other parent’s prior consent is required. The Court of Appeal identified no statutory provision permitting substitution of the known and living mother’s consent with that of an ad hoc guardian under the full-adoption regime. Article 30(4), read with Article 41 SAPVAA, was thereby incorrectly applied. ### 5. The second-instance court confused Articles 30 and 31 of the Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina In the part of its reasoning rejecting the central ground of appeal, the Court of Appeal states: > “During the proceedings and in his grounds of appeal, the plaintiff mainly argues that the conditions in Article 31 of the aforementioned Act were not fulfilled for establishing his full adoption on 21 June 1979 (...). Paragraph 1 of that provision stipulates that a child below the age of five whose parents are dead or unknown may be fully adopted...” This is incorrect. The content the Court of Appeal attributes to Article 31 is actually the content of Article 30(1) SAPVAA. Article 31 does not govern the status of the child’s parents. It provides that full adoption may be established only by both spouses. This error is not an insignificant technical slip in a peripheral part of the reasoning. It occurs precisely where the second-instance court attempts to answer the main ground of appeal concerning incorrect application of Article 30. A court that rejects the central ground of appeal concerning Article 30 by incorrectly identifying the provision itself, and then avoids determining whether it applies paragraph 1 or paragraph 4, has not correctly applied substantive law. ### 6. Thus the plaintiff’s grounds of appeal alleging that it is unclear whether his case was brought under paragraph 1 or paragraph 4 of the cited provision are not acceptable, bearing in mind that, in the situation described, the conditions for adoption relating to the status of the plaintiff’s parents were in any event met. The first-instance court correctly finds that the guardianship body established fulfilment of all the other conditions necessary for full adoption, which also concern assessment of the prospective adopters by professionals—an educational specialist, psychologist, social worker and doctor. This court finds no inconsistencies in their findings, and the grounds of appeal advanced in that respect are not acceptable.
