Court filing / Foundational document

Statement of Claim

Opening court filing challenging the 1979 full adoption as an unlawful act of identity substitution imposed on a child of Hungarian-Croatian origin, with continuing consequences borne within the legal continuity of the Republic of Serbia.

Higher Court in Novi Sad 2024-02-22
This page is the public entry point to the opening claim: why the 1979 act is attacked as unlawful, how biological continuity and minority-origin identity were cut through institutionally enabled substitution, and why those consequences remain active today within the legal continuity of the Republic of Serbia.

Article 284 · Preservation of evidence

Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Serbia

The claim requests urgent examination of Vinka Ignjatić to preserve her evidence. Article 284 permits evidence to be taken before or during proceedings where there is a justified risk that later examination would be impossible or difficult; preservation proceedings are urgent.

Maja Petrović’s CSR submission opposes annulment and dismisses the suitability criticism — closing date 7 May 2025

Related source

The submission expressly rejects the claim’s assessment criticism as sweeping, unqualified and inadmissible after 46 years. The linked full claim supplies the passages being challenged, including the reports’ content, Cvita Miletić’s consent and search, and the author’s account of beatings, hostility, witnessed violence and abandonment. Taking all of the above into account, the sweeping allegations in the narrative of the claim are unfounded and cannot be given weight, and especially those allegations which, as grounds for the nullity

Source page 3

of the adoption, assert that Gospava and Milorad Vasić were unsuitable for adoption, because in the narrative of the claim that assessment is made by unqualified persons who have no statutory authority to do so. It is inadmissible to interpret findings and opinions from a distance of 46 years and to state “that the checks on the suitability of those persons were not carried out properly, if any checks were carried out at all, since the defendant’s reports on the suitability of the adopters contain a series of stock phrases repeated over and over in all similar reports and situations”.

It is undisputed from all of the above that the procedure in question was conducted in accordance with the legally prescribed procedures and professional standards applicable at the time the adoption ruling was issued, and that there is not a single statutory ground for its annulment.
