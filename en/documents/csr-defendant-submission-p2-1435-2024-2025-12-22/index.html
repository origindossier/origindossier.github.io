Maja Petrović: selected statements from Vera’s dossier used to defend adoption — CSR submission dated 22 December 2025

CSR lists archive documents and treats Vera’s statements about parentage and care as undisputed grounds for adoption. It dates the quotation to 6 April, whereas the confirmed transcription records 8 April; withdrawal of probate representation becomes a conclusion about relatives’ lack of care. The party labels are again reversed. Dated 22 December; received by the court on 24 December 2025.

Novi Sad Social Work Centre 2025-12-22
Maja Petrović signs CSR’s submission relying on dossier 3871/79 and Judge Bojana Vujić’s order of 26 November 2025. It lists seven items. The thirteen historical scans correspond to existing pages 1–13 of Vera’s dossier; the reverse of the personal record, with information about the mother, is page 14 of the existing complete source. The submission links unestablished legal paternity, old statements about Cvita’s life and Helena’s withdrawal from probate representation to the assertion that no relatives wanted to care for the child. The same text then identifies the father’s sister who assumed care for payment. Comparative annotations open the original records of the known and acknowledged father, insurance through the mother, the child’s condition, family care and the child’s crying over the change of name. The concluding defence states that no grounds for annulment existed without relating that conclusion to the separate adoption requirements challenged by the author. His later assessment of this submission as crowning evidence appears in a separate author’s note. The public PDF contains the first two new pages; historical attachments are accessed through the existing full dossier. The submission date of 22 December differs from the court receipt on 24 December 2025.

BASIC COURT IN NOVI SAD
Case no.: P2.1435/24
PLAINTIFF: Milan Petrović (born as Milivoj Miletić), Kraljevačkog bataljona 110, Kragujevac
DEFENDANT: Centre for Social Work of the City of Novi Sad, Zmaj Ognjena Vuka 13, Novi Sad

PLAINTIFF’S SUBMISSION

(In response to defendant’s submission no. 560-111-1/2024 of 22 December 2025; supplementation of proposed evidence and legal reasoning)

I. INTRODUCTION AND PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK

By submission no. 560-111-1/2024 of 22 December 2025, the defendant supplies certain archive documents (the dossier cover, foster-carer questionnaire, contracts and decisions on family placement, case-monitoring sheet and beneficiary’s personal record), and draws the following conclusions from those documents:

  • that, as a child, I was “without parental care”;
  • that allegedly “none of the close relatives” wanted to assume care of me;
  • that adoption was “the best form of protection under family law”;
  • that “there were no grounds for annulling the adoption at the time it was established” and “everything complied with the regulations then in force”.

At the same time, the defendant has still not complied with all this Court’s orders, particularly those requiring submission of:

  • the complete documentation concerning the acquisition of authority over the case (“how the child came under its authority”); and
  • the complete file on the full adoption in 1979,

in accordance with:

  1. the minutes and orders from the hearing of 26 March 2025; and
  2. the Court’s decision at the hearing of 26 November 2025 (the fifteen- and thirty-day periods).

Through this submission, I:

  1. respond to the assertions in the defendant’s submission of 22 December 2025;
  2. point out the selective and incorrect interpretation of the documentation supplied;
  3. specify the nature of the violation — deprivation of identity, forced assimilation (Serbianization), and the fact that this is a continuing act;
  4. propose that, because of the persistent failure to supply crucial acts, the Court draw adverse evidentiary inferences regarding the disputed points.

Note: This submission is presented as a procedural act in these proceedings, but was also written to reconstruct the facts and institutional conduct comprehensively through evidence, bearing in mind that the consequences of the contested act constitute a continuing violation and that the contents of the judgment and case file will matter beyond a single courtroom.

Why this source is linked

The December submission quotes doubt about Pál’s paternity, then refers to the natural father’s death and his sister. In the linked P.1642/76 divorce record, Pál states that he had a son with Cvita, and his wife Vera Lakatoš also describes that child. These are concrete records of knowledge and acknowledgment, distinct from formal judicial establishment of paternity.

“Establishing the circumstances of prenatal and postnatal development—during pregnancy the mother led a disorderly life. After childbirth he lived with his mother for a short time, when she abandoned him. He was brought up by Pál Lakatoš, who claims to be the child’s natural father, but this is unlikely because Cvita lived with several men at the same time.”

“Establishing the manifestations of the origin and development of the problem: after the death of the child’s natural father, the child was left without parental care.”

Based on the entry of 16 April 1979, it is established that care of the minor Milivoj was taken over by Pál Lakatoš’s sister, Julka Vizmet, who lived in Irig, and that she agreed to care for the child for payment as a foster carer, which constitutes a temporary form of protection.
