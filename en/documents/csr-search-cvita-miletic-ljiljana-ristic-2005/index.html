Ljiljana Ristić’s 2005 report recommending an end to the search for Cvita Miletić

On 9 March 2005, Ljiljana Ristić recommends stopping the search for Cvita Miletić and says that a meeting with her son should not take place. The report checks Novi Sad death records only from 1 June 1998, leaving the author’s stated 1997 death outside that period.

Novi Sad Social Work Centre 2005-03-09
Ljiljana Ristić signs the social worker’s report of 9 March 2005 concerning the search requested by Milan Petrović on 28 July 2004. Its final recommendation is explicit: the biological mother and her adopted son should not meet, and further searches for Cvita should be stopped. The report identifies Cvita Miletić as born on 23 March 1931 in Bila Vlaka, Benkovac, SR Croatia. It records an address at 138 Žarka Zrenjanina Street in Temerin, where the police patrol was told that Cvita was not known. The Temerin death-register check is negative. For Novi Sad, the stated death-register period begins on 1 June 1998 and runs to the date of the report. The text describes Cvita’s earlier behaviour as psychologically altered, without citing a medical finding. Searches at the psychiatric hospitals in Vršac and Kovin produced no record of her. Despite not locating Cvita, Ljiljana Ristić assesses that she is not in a better condition “today” and uses that assessment in recommending that the search and meeting should be abandoned. The birthplace is given as SR Croatia, but the explanation for the failed Benkovac inquiry invokes the wartime failure to forward data about people originating from Bosnia and Herzegovina. These are different geographical grounds within the same report. Author’s later information: Cvita died on 15 June 1997; the author learned this in 2021. Damjan Đukić told him in 2021 that Cvita had lived with him in 1996–1997, then went to Melenci and died. The report contains neither the 1997 death nor Melenci. Its Novi Sad search period starting in 1998 excludes the author’s stated year of death. The author regards the report’s ending as an arrogant withholding of information and obstruction of his search for his mother.

Ljiljana Ristić recommends stopping the search for Cvita Miletić
Why this source is linked
Related source

The report dated 9 March 2005 records social worker Ljiljana Ristić’s recommendation to stop the search for Cvita Miletić.

On the basis of the information presented, it can be concluded that further attempts to locate Miletić Cvita exceed the capabilities of this authority. It should also be borne in mind that Cvita was in a very poor psychophysical and socioeconomic condition when she was receiving protection from the Centre for Social Work in Novi Sad. The assessment is that even today she is not in a better condition, which significantly determines the response to her son's request.

It is my opinion that this meeting between the biological mother and the adopted son should not take place and that further searches for Cvita should be discontinued.
