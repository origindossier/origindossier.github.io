Exclusion of Milivoj Miletić from O.196/79 — Pervaz appeal, adoption secrecy and court legalization of contaminated material

This is the Pervaz node in the inheritance face of identity substitution: the private attorney for the heirs uses the confidential full-adoption route and alleged prospective-adopter statements against the child's already recognized inheritance position. The court does not examine the source of official secrecy, does not separate the contaminated appellate material, and does not protect the child; it accepts the appeal as a lawful procedural basis.

Municipal Court / Higher Court in Novi Sad 1980-11-10
Forensic function: Jovan Pervaz, private attorney for the heirs, on 12 Dec 1979 inserts the full-adoption route of Milivoj Miletić and alleged statements of prospective adopters into the appeal. Those data belong to a closed adoption procedure, not to the probate side represented by Pervaz. The appeal therefore becomes a contaminated procedural act: it uses official adoption secrecy against a child who had already been placed inside the inheritance frame of Pavle / Pál Lakatoš. In a rule-of-law state this would open disciplinary proceedings before the bar association, questioning about the source of confidential data, and exclusion or rejection of the appellate line based on official secrecy. Instead, the court receives that appeal as lawful material and builds on it the further route for removing the child from the heir circle.

8. The second-instance court incorrectly assessed the inheritance and paternal trail as legally irrelevant background

The lawfulness of the 1979 full adoption cannot be assessed in isolation from the parallel probate proceedings after the death of Pavle Lakatoš, in which the minor Milivoj Miletić appears as a child whose rights and interests must be protected.

In this context, the following facts are decisive:

  • in the record of 19 March 1979, the minor Milivoj Miletić is recorded as a child staying with his grandparents, the parents of the deceased Pavle Lakatoš;
  • on 20 July 1979, the Municipal Court in Novi Sad seeks appointment of a guardian to protect his rights and interests in the probate proceedings;
  • by the decision of 23 November 1979 he is declared an heir after Pavle Lakatoš;
  • in appeal proceedings Gž 11/80, the other heirs possess information about the full-adoption proceedings, including the assertion that the adopters do not object to Milivoj inheriting from anyone, and use that material against his inheritance position.

This shows that the case files simultaneously contained:

  • an adoption track portraying the child as eligible for complete displacement of identity;
  • a probate track in which the same child appears as Milivoj Miletić, connected with Pavle Lakatoš’s paternal line and the exercise of inheritance rights.

The Court of Appeal reduced this conflict to the subsequent setting aside of the inheritance decision and thereby avoided assessing its significance for the lawfulness of the full adoption of 21 June 1979.

This is an incorrect application of substantive law: the conditions for full adoption must be assessed against the legal framework and facts at the time it was established, not against the later removal of one of the traces revealing those facts.

9. The second-instance court did not assess the legal significance of Articles 35, 37 and 38 of the Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina

Article 35 SAPVAA provides that the report establishing full adoption contains information about the birth entry being cancelled and information for the adoptee’s new birth entry, identifying the adopters as parents.

Article 37 provides that full adoption terminates the statutory rights and duties between the adoptee and his blood relatives.

Article 38 provides that determination of the paternity and maternity of a fully adopted child is not permitted.

These provisions show the actual effect of full adoption.

They confirm that the contested act did not merely change a surname or family placement.

In other words, the first-instance court formally cited Article 30 but did not establish the decisive facts without which that provision cannot be applied: neither the legal framework governing this particular case, nor the duration and course of the search, nor a lawful basis for substituting the special-purpose guardian’s consent for that of a known mother.

3. Pavle Lakatoš and the traces of paternity were not properly assessed

The court reduced the issue of Pavle Lakatoš to the simple observation that paternity had not been judicially established. This bypassed the substance of the matter.

The proceedings raised and documented traces showing that Pavle Lakatoš:

  • acknowledged the child in the context of divorce proceedings;
  • that the family knew of the child’s existence;
  • that I lived within my paternal family circle;
  • that I was staying with my paternal grandmother and grandfather;
  • that my status as the deceased’s son born outside marriage was discussed in the probate proceedings;
  • that at one point I was even declared an heir.

The court did not assess the significance of these traces for whether the administrative formula “father unknown” reflected the actual facts, or represented an administrative reduction of a more complex family and inheritance reality to facilitate full adoption.

4. Probate proceedings O.196/79 were not properly assessed

The probate proceedings were not incidental background, but one of the central pieces of evidence.

They contain:

  • the record of 19 March 1979, stating that the minor Milivoj was staying with his grandparents, namely the deceased’s parents;
  • the letter of 20 July 1979, requesting the appointment of a guardian to protect his rights and interests in the probate proceedings;
  • appellate proceedings Gž 11/80, showing that private heirs possessed information that full-adoption proceedings had taken place before SIZ and that the adopters had stated they did not seek for Milivoj to inherit from anyone.

The court did not explain how the same child could exist in one legal channel as Miletić Milivoj, a potential heir of Pavle Lakatoš, and in another as a child without parental care, ready for full adoption and the rewriting of his identity into another family.

Nor did the judgment explain why it did not consider that material decisive, although it directly concerns identity, inheritance status and the timing of institutional action towards the child.

5. The second adoption in 1984 was left without legal explanation

The judgment states that I was adopted by the Petrović couple in 1984 and was assigned their surname, but does not clarify how this was possible at all if a full adoption had already been lawfully established in 1979.
