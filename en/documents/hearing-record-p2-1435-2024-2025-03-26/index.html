Bojana Vujić’s hearing: “how he came under their jurisdiction”, CSR opposition and an order for adoption records — 26 March 2025

The plaintiff personally requests witnesses, divorce records and documents showing how he came under CSR jurisdiction. Maja Petrović opposes pre-adoption family-law-protection files and the divorce evidence, requests another 15 days for the Act and her position, and supplies the 2005 search report. The panel orders adoption documentation within 15 days and schedules 5 June.

Basic Court in Novi Sad 2025-03-26
At the non-public hearing of 26 March 2025, P2.1435/24, Bojana Vujić presides; Milan Petrović appears personally and Maja Petrović represents the defendant CSR. Because the panel composition has changed, the proceedings resume by reading the case file. Page 2 nevertheless states that the defendant’s submission dated 10 March is handed to the “plaintiff’s representative”; the full translation retains this inconsistency with his personal appearance. The plaintiff maintains his claim and proposes Vinka Ignjatić and her son Dragan as witnesses to the adopters’ dysfunctional marriage before the full adoption. He seeks his examination as a party, the divorce record and file P.1106/82, and documents explaining how he “came under their jurisdiction”. He withdraws his request for Gospava Vasić’s medical history. He explains that he had not properly understood the earlier 30-day task concerning witnesses and maintains the remaining proposed witnesses. Maja Petrović opposes the claim, the witnesses and obtaining the divorce file, arguing that grounds for annulment must exist when the adoption is established and that the proposed evidence is not relevant. She requests another 15 days to provide the SAP Vojvodina Adoption Act and a detailed written position. She supplies the social worker’s 9 March 2005 report about searching for Cvita Miletić; a photocopy is placed in the file and handed to the plaintiff, who says he already has it. On page 3, the plaintiff agrees to her requested period and says he will supply the Act himself. Maja Petrović does not oppose supplying records of the establishment of the adoption but explicitly opposes pre-adoption family-law-protection files explaining “how he came under our jurisdiction”, saying they would burden the case and questioning their relevance. The panel’s final order requires a detailed written response, appropriate evidence and all adoption documentation held by the defendant within 15 days of announcement. It schedules 5 June 2025 at 10:00 on the parties’ joint proposal; that future hearing is not presented as held. This hearing ends at 10:30. In a separately marked later clarification, the author states: “I already had the same Act and had been offering it to the court throughout.” This is his later statement, alongside the record’s entry that he would also supply it himself. He also believes that a copy of the Act accompanied his claim as an attachment.

Article 118 · Reading and correcting the hearing record

Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Serbia

The form’s printed reference to “particulars under Article 118(1)” does not match the current Act’s numbering. Those particulars are in Article 116(1), to which the current small-claims provision, Article 474, expressly refers. The printed numbering corresponds to Articles 118(1) and 473 of the 2004 Act. This is evidence of an outdated reference in the form; it does not establish that the court applied the former Act or classified this identity/adoption dispute as a small claim.

The defendant’s representative states that she opposes the action and the claim in their entirety because the statutory conditions for annulment of the full adoption applicable when it was concluded are not fulfilled. She requests an additional period of 15 days, during which I will provide a detailed written statement and supply the Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina containing the provisions. At today’s hearing I submit the social worker’s report in the search procedure for Cvita Miletić, dated 09.03.2005. I oppose the evidentiary proposals to examine all the proposed witnesses about the dysfunctional marriage of the adopters Gospava and Milorad Vasić, since this is not a ground for annulment of the full adoption and such testimony by the proposed witnesses is questionable because of their indirect knowledge of the family circumstances. I agree that the plaintiff should be examined as a party and that the attached written documentation should be inspected, and I request 15 days to provide a detailed written statement and supply the then applicable Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina which applied at the time of this plaintiff’s full adoption. She also opposes officially requesting inspection of the divorce litigation between the late adopters Gospava and Milorad Vasić, because it and the statements contained therein are not relevant to these proceedings for annulment of the full adoption, since the grounds for annulment must exist at the time the full adoption was established.

BASIC COURT IN NOVI SAD
Case no.: P2.1435/24
PLAINTIFF: Milan Petrović (born as Milivoj Miletić), Kraljevačkog bataljona 110, Kragujevac
DEFENDANT: Centre for Social Work of the City of Novi Sad, Zmaj Ognjena Vuka 13, Novi Sad

PLAINTIFF’S SUBMISSION

(In response to defendant’s submission no. 560-111-1/2024 of 22 December 2025; supplementation of proposed evidence and legal reasoning)

I. INTRODUCTION AND PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK

By submission no. 560-111-1/2024 of 22 December 2025, the defendant supplies certain archive documents (the dossier cover, foster-carer questionnaire, contracts and decisions on family placement, case-monitoring sheet and beneficiary’s personal record), and draws the following conclusions from those documents:

  • that, as a child, I was “without parental care”;
  • that allegedly “none of the close relatives” wanted to assume care of me;
  • that adoption was “the best form of protection under family law”;
  • that “there were no grounds for annulling the adoption at the time it was established” and “everything complied with the regulations then in force”.

At the same time, the defendant has still not complied with all this Court’s orders, particularly those requiring submission of:

  • the complete documentation concerning the acquisition of authority over the case (“how the child came under its authority”); and
  • the complete file on the full adoption in 1979,

in accordance with:

  1. the minutes and orders from the hearing of 26 March 2025; and
  2. the Court’s decision at the hearing of 26 November 2025 (the fifteen- and thirty-day periods).

Through this submission, I:

  1. respond to the assertions in the defendant’s submission of 22 December 2025;
  2. point out the selective and incorrect interpretation of the documentation supplied;
  3. specify the nature of the violation — deprivation of identity, forced assimilation (Serbianization), and the fact that this is a continuing act;
  4. propose that, because of the persistent failure to supply crucial acts, the Court draw adverse evidentiary inferences regarding the disputed points.

Note: This submission is presented as a procedural act in these proceedings, but was also written to reconstruct the facts and institutional conduct comprehensively through evidence, bearing in mind that the consequences of the contested act constitute a continuing violation and that the contents of the judgment and case file will matter beyond a single courtroom.
