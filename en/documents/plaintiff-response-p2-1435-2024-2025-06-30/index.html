Case submission / Primary source

Plaintiff’s response of 30 June 2025: obstruction, backdating and nullity of the 1979 adoption

Milan Petrović responds to the CSR’s submission, challenges its reversed party labels and failure to provide documentation, and argues backdating and the nullity of the full adoption. He cites the unanswered settlement proposal and expressly lists the 1976 Act as attachment 3.

Basic Court in Novi Sad 2025-06-30
In his submission dated 30 June 2025, Milan Petrović (born as Milivoj Miletić) challenges the CSR’s treatment of the case and its failure to attend the hearing scheduled for 5 June. He describes the 26 March order as requiring complete documentation of how the Centre acquired authority over him. He argues that the 19 March 1979 probate record, the 20 July guardianship order and the 21 June adoption decision demonstrate backdating and the nullity of the full adoption, and sets out violations of the 1976 Adoption Act. He cites contradictions and falsifications in the professional assessments, the absence of a genuine search for his mother and the disregard of parental consent, identity and inheritance rights. He asks the court to declare the adoption null and void and record the defendant’s continued disregard of court orders and settlement proposals. The submission lists five attachments: the 20 July 1979 court order, the 19 March probate record, the 1976 Adoption Act, the 3 March 2025 settlement proposal with proof of receipt, and the analysis of the June 1979 professional reports. The three supplied pages list these attachments but do not contain them. The date is the document’s date, without a filing or receipt stamp. Its allegations and requests remain the plaintiff’s statements.

Article 2 · The child's interest

SAPV Adoption Act 1976 - legal mechanism map

The plaintiff invokes the child-interest condition in his argument about identity and inheritance. Article 2 states that adoption may be established only if it is in the child’s interests. This is the named statutory source for his argument.

Short quotation from the source
- Article 2: Adoption may be established only if it is in the child’s interests, which is not the case here because the child’s fundamental rights to identity and inheritance were violated.

Article 5 · Consent of parents or guardian

SAPV Adoption Act 1976 - legal mechanism map

The plaintiff alleges that his living mother’s consent was never sought. Article 5 requires parental consent and separately specifies circumstances in which a guardian gives consent. The linked text provides both paragraphs, rather than reducing the provision to the plaintiff’s short description.

Short quotation from the source
- Article 5: Parental consent is required. My mother was alive, at a known address, but her consent was never sought, nor was a genuine search conducted. The Centre’s claims that my mother’s address was unknown are demonstrably untrue.

Article 11 · Persons disqualified from adopting

SAPV Adoption Act 1976 - legal mechanism map

The plaintiff links adopter suitability to dysfunction and violence. Article 11 sets out particular prohibitions, including a founded suspicion that the adopter will use the position to the child’s detriment and disease endangering the child’s health or life. The reference points to those actual conditions, without replacing the plaintiff’s argument with a medical finding.

Short quotation from the source
- Article 11: Adopters must be suitable. Court and other evidence confirm serious dysfunction, violence and psychological problems on the part of the adopters, which the Centre disregarded.

Article 28 · Court jurisdiction and standing for annulment

SAPV Adoption Act 1976 - legal mechanism map

The plaintiff cites Article 28 together with Article 41 in asking for annulment. Article 28 assigns the annulment decision to the district court and names the persons entitled to bring the action. The reference preserves the statute’s actual procedural wording rather than presenting the plaintiff’s requested outcome as an existing judgment.

- Articles 28 and 41: The court has the right and duty to declare an adoption null and void if it was not carried out in accordance with the law. In this case, there are multiple legal grounds for the adoption’s nullity.

Article 30 · Conditions for full adoption

SAPV Adoption Act 1976 - legal mechanism map

The plaintiff invokes the full-adoption age and parental-status conditions. The actual first paragraph also requires that the whereabouts of parents who abandoned the child remain undetermined after six months; paragraph 4 covers one deceased or unknown parent with the other parent’s prior consent. Paragraphs 2–3 separately regulate conversion of an earlier adoption. The full article is linked for the precise conditions used in his nullity argument.

Short quotation from the source
- Article 30: Full adoption is possible only if the parents have died, are unknown, or have abandoned the child for more than six months. This condition was not fulfilled here, because my mother was alive and could have been found.

The judgment gives no genuine reasons addressing any of these questions. These are not reasons for a judgment, but a bare declaration. It is especially significant that the first-instance court incorporated into the reasoning of the challenged judgment the fact that the plaintiff was adopted again in 1984 by another couple, without explaining that fact in legal terms. The court thereby incorporated into its own reasoning a fact that destabilises its own conclusion that the full adoption in 1979 was lawfully and permanently established, and then left that fact without any legal analysis.
