Second adoption record - Vasić Milan transferred into Petrović Milan

CSR Novi Sad executes the second adoption through Vasić Milan, the identity produced by the contested 1979 full-adoption record. The proceeding does not return to Milivoj Miletić, Cvita Miletić, Pavle / Pal Lakatoš or the probate trace.

Novi Sad Social Work Centre 1984-09-26
This is the operative record of the double adoption loop. CSR Novi Sad does not return the child to the real identity line. It conducts the second adoption through Vasić Milan, the identity manufactured by the first adoption. Article 2 requires the child’s interest; Article 7 defines who may adopt and how spouses adopt; Articles 14 and 15 require preparatory verification and professional opinions; Article 17 requires rejection if the conditions or child’s interest fail; Articles 18 and 19 govern the hearing and consent; Article 21 requires the record to contain the acts and statements; Article 22 sends the adoption record to the registrar; Article 24 confirms that ordinary adoption does not terminate blood kinship and obligations. The record turns a manufactured 1979 identity into the procedural foundation for a 1984 transfer.

3. Pavle Lakatoš and the traces of paternity were not properly assessed

The court reduced the issue of Pavle Lakatoš to the simple observation that paternity had not been judicially established. This bypassed the substance of the matter.

The proceedings raised and documented traces showing that Pavle Lakatoš:

  • acknowledged the child in the context of divorce proceedings;
  • that the family knew of the child’s existence;
  • that I lived within my paternal family circle;
  • that I was staying with my paternal grandmother and grandfather;
  • that my status as the deceased’s son born outside marriage was discussed in the probate proceedings;
  • that at one point I was even declared an heir.

The court did not assess the significance of these traces for whether the administrative formula “father unknown” reflected the actual facts, or represented an administrative reduction of a more complex family and inheritance reality to facilitate full adoption.

4. Probate proceedings O.196/79 were not properly assessed

The probate proceedings were not incidental background, but one of the central pieces of evidence.

They contain:

  • the record of 19 March 1979, stating that the minor Milivoj was staying with his grandparents, namely the deceased’s parents;
  • the letter of 20 July 1979, requesting the appointment of a guardian to protect his rights and interests in the probate proceedings;
  • appellate proceedings Gž 11/80, showing that private heirs possessed information that full-adoption proceedings had taken place before SIZ and that the adopters had stated they did not seek for Milivoj to inherit from anyone.

The court did not explain how the same child could exist in one legal channel as Miletić Milivoj, a potential heir of Pavle Lakatoš, and in another as a child without parental care, ready for full adoption and the rewriting of his identity into another family.

Nor did the judgment explain why it did not consider that material decisive, although it directly concerns identity, inheritance status and the timing of institutional action towards the child.

5. The second adoption in 1984 was left without legal explanation

The judgment states that I was adopted by the Petrović couple in 1984 and was assigned their surname, but does not clarify how this was possible at all if a full adoption had already been lawfully established in 1979.

Revision of 29 May 2026: the appellate judgment confronted with the 1976 law and the identity evidence
Why this source is linked
Related source

The 1984 adoption is invoked as evidence bearing on the premise of a lawful, indissoluble 1979 adoption. The revision expressly does not substitute a separate claim to annul the second act for the existing claim.

“Full adoption may not be dissolved.”

In the factual part of its judgment, the Court of Appeal accepts that:

  • on 21 June 1979 the plaintiff was fully adopted by Milorad and Gospava Vasić;
  • on 26 September 1984 the plaintiff was adopted again by Periša and Georgina Petrović.

If the first full adoption was lawful and indissoluble, a second adoption of the same child could not lawfully have been carried out.

If the second adoption was nevertheless carried out, that cannot have no bearing on assessment of the legal sustainability of the first full adoption.

The second-instance court refused to draw any conclusion from this direct statutory contradiction, reasoning that the validity of the second adoption is not the subject of the proceedings.

That approach is wrong.

The subject of the proceedings is the nullity of the 1979 full adoption.

The fact that the same system placed the same child for adoption again in 1984 is directly relevant to assessing whether the 1979 act was indeed lawfully, fully and permanently established.

The Court of Appeal neutralised the legal significance of this fact instead of assessing it.

It thereby failed to apply Article 40 and assess the status consequences under Articles 35, 37 and 38 SAPVAA.

7. The second-instance court incorrectly applied Article 34 of the Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina

Article 34 SAPVAA provides:

“Information about the adoptee’s parents shall not be entered in the record establishing full adoption.”

Yet the Court of Appeal states in its reasoning that the full-adoption record Up.03-507/79 of 21 June 1979 records:

  • that the minor Milivoj’s mother has been at an unknown place of residence for a prolonged period;
  • that she does not perform her parental duties without justifiable reasons;
  • that Vera Radovanović was appointed ad hoc guardian and consents to adoption.

In other words, according to the Court of Appeal’s own factual description, the full-adoption record contains information about the adoptee’s parent, and uses it as the central factual basis for establishing full adoption.

That directly contradicts Article 34 SAPVAA.

The Court of Appeal did not assess this legal consequence at all.

Although this violation was not separately numbered in the appeal, it follows from the contested judgment’s own factual description and is a legal consequence of the facts the second-instance court itself accepted as established.

It is therefore directly relevant in proceedings on this appeal on points of law, as further confirmation that the lower courts applied SAPVAA selectively.

VI. DOUBLE ADOPTION, DISREGARD OF THE NEGATIVE 1984 ASSESSMENT, AND FALSE “PATERNAL CONSENT”

On several occasions, including the earlier submission of 27 November 2025, the defendant supplies the file on incomplete adoption no. UP 02-987/84 concerning Periša and Gorgina Petrović, while stating that it considers that file “irrelevant”, because formally this action does not seek annulment of that adoption.

However, the very facts:

  • that full adoption by Vasić Milorad and Gospava was already established in 1979, which the defendant does not dispute; and
  • that, in 1984, the same body — the Centre for Social Work in Novi Sad — participated in a second adoption by different adoptive parents,

are directly contrary to Article 7 of the Adoption Act of the Socialist Autonomous Province of Vojvodina then in force (1976), which provides that multiple persons cannot adopt one person unless they are married to one another. An already established full adoption cannot be “transferred” to a new married couple — that is precisely why it is called full adoption.

Thus, the 1984 incomplete-adoption file is not irrelevant: it is evidence of the continuity of the defendant’s unlawful conduct:

  • first, full adoption in 1979 before the fifth birthday, without fulfilling the conditions under Article 30 — without the mother’s consent, effectively disregarding Pal Lakatoš’s paternity and probate proceedings O.196/79;
  • then “readoption” in 1984 by different adoptive parents, although full adoption already existed and would, under the law, exclude another round of adoption.
