Case submission / Primary source

Maja Petrović’s CSR submission opposes annulment and dismisses the suitability criticism — closing date 7 May 2025

The CSR asserts that the adoption met every statutory requirement, cites Articles 41, 4, 10, 30, 31 and 11 of the 1976 Act, and lists ten evidentiary items. It dismisses criticism of Gospava and Milorad Vasić’s suitability as coming from unqualified persons without statutory authority and as interpretation from a distance of 46 years. The heading gives 1 April; the closing text gives 7 May 2025.

Novi Sad Social Work Centre 2025-05-07
In case P2.1435/24, the Centre for Social Work of the City of Novi Sad opposes annulment of the adoption. Its submission, reference 560-111-1/2024, bears 1 April 2025 in the heading and 7 May 2025 in its closing text, above Maja Petrović’s signature. The chronology uses the closing date; the supplied pages do not separately establish the actual date of signing, filing or receipt. The heading reverses the party labels, calling the CSR the plaintiff and Milan Petrović the defendant. The opening paragraph nevertheless identifies the guardianship authority as the defendant, and the signature formula reads “For the defendant”. This contradiction remains visible in the full translation. The CSR invokes Article 41 and reproduces provisions it identifies as Articles 4(2)–(3), 10(1), 30(1), (2) and (4), 31 and 11 of the Adoption Act adopted by the Assembly of SAP Vojvodina on 23 September 1976. The incomplete Article 30 construction across pages 1 and 2 is retained as the submission’s text. The CSR declares it undisputed that all statutory rules were followed through suitability assessments by a social worker, an educational specialist and a psychologist and the required documentation. Besides a copy of the Act, it lists ten items: a birth-register extract, a marriage-register extract, two medical certificates, two Municipal Court certificates, an employment-and-income certificate from “Novograp”, and the psychologist’s, social worker’s and educational specialist’s June 1979 reports. The attachments themselves are not contained in these three supplied pages. The closing argument rejects the claim’s suitability criticism as sweeping and unfounded, says it is made by “unqualified persons who have no statutory authority to do so”, and calls interpretation of the findings from a distance of 46 years inadmissible. It quotes the criticism of recurring stock phrases, then asserts that no statutory ground for annulment exists. These are the CSR’s assertions, signed in Maja Petrović’s name, not a judicial determination.
