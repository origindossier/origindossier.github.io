Vasić pre-adoption assessment (June 1979) — compressed validation of the first identity substitution

The package from 12 to 21 June 1979 shows accelerated expert validation of complete adoption: the social-work report, pedagogical opinion, psychological opinion, appointment of a special guardian and complete adoption are compressed into nine days. That tempo ends with a name change and the child’s civil-register rewriting.

Social Protection SIZ in Novi Sad 1979-06-19
This package is not a neutral assessment of adoptive suitability. From 12 to 21 June 1979, the social-work, pedagogical, psychological, guardianship and civil-register apparatus assemble an accelerated track that ends with the complete adoption of Miletić Milivoj, the change of name to Vasić Milan, and the insertion of the Vasić couple as parents. Vera Radovanović’s social-work report of 12 June proposes approval of the adoption. The pedagogical opinion of 18 June repeats the construction that the child is without parental care, with an unknown father and a mother at an unknown place of residence, although the earlier dossier 3871/79 already carried traces of Cvita, Pal Lakatoš and Vizmeg Julka as the sister of father Pal. The psychological opinion of 19 June gives a positive conclusion even though it records that Gospava does not know how to read and that verbal tests therefore could not be applied. Already on 20 June Vera Radovanović becomes the special guardian for consent, and on 21 June consent is given, complete adoption is declared, the child is renamed, and the path is opened for erasing the previous data in the birth register. The dates show compressed execution of the first identity substitution, not orderly expert preparation. The package also builds a socialist suitability profile of the adopters. Gospava is legitimized through war, camp suffering, loss of parents, work and self-reliance, although the same package is internally inconsistent: one report states that her mother died in 1955 and her father was killed in Jasenovac in 1942, while the pedagogical report later writes as if both parents were killed in a camp. Milorad is presented through a worker’s ascent from unqualified labourer to skilled worker and brigadier, with the formula that he enjoys the reputation of a good worker, man and comrade. This moral-political profile of the adults displaces the forensic question of the child: Milivoj’s distorted parental status, Gospava’s illiteracy, the compressed dates and the civil-register consequence of complete adoption are not treated as central risks. Legal framework: Articles 2, 14, 15, 17, 30, 31, 41 and 42 of the 1976 SAPV Adoption Act.

Appeal of 18 March 2026: nullity, unanswered evidence and the second adoption
Why this source is linked
Related source

The appeal expressly challenges formulaic and mutually inconsistent professional assessments. The linked June 1979 assessment dossier contains the three reports concerning the Vasić couple; it is distinct from the later Petrović assessment.

The court was required to explain how a new adoption in 1984 was possible if a valid full adoption already existed in 1979, one that could not be dissolved under the law then in force. By failing to do so, it failed to apply substantive law to a decisive fact that directly undermines the conclusion that the first adoption was lawful. #### 4. Wrongly reducing the dispute to a mere family-law framework The court treated the case as a typical family dispute, although it is a dispute against the guardianship body concerning the lawfulness of its historical and present conduct, identity, origins, inheritance status and the consequences of an administrative change of identity. The first-instance court thereby wrongly marginalised the identity dimension of the dispute, although precisely that dimension determines a proper understanding of the legal significance of the contested adoption and the subsequent institutional maintenance of its consequences. ### VI. INCORRECT ASSESSMENT OF EVIDENCE The first-instance court did not critically assess the contents of the archive documentation. Instead, it effectively equated its formal authenticity and official provenance with the truth of its contents and the lawfulness of the conduct of the body that created it. It accepted particularly uncritically the documents created by the very body whose conduct is contested. Nor did the first-instance court critically assess the fact that the defendant’s internal documentation contains a degrading narrative permeated with value judgments and sex-based and cultural prejudices about my biological mother. Instead, it effectively accepted that content without genuine legal and evidentiary scrutiny. It thereby did more than uncritically adopt the defendant’s internal documentation: it indirectly provided a legitimising framework for descriptions contaminated by value judgments that could not be treated as a neutral and reliable basis for judicial conclusions. The contradictions and problems concerning the following were not properly assessed: - the monitoring sheet of contacts and casework; - social worker Vera Radovanović’s report; - formulaic and mutually inconsistent professional assessments; - moral characterisations of the mother based on “consultation with neighbours”, without a serious evidentiary basis; - the fact that CSR’s own internal documentation confirms in several places that the child was well cared for, tidy, staying with paternal relatives and undergoing accelerated psychological preparation for an identity change. In other words, the court did not compare and weigh conflicting evidence, but privileged the defendant’s internal documentation in advance. ### VII. DECISION ON COSTS I also challenge the decision that each party is to bear its own costs.
