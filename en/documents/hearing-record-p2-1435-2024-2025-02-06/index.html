Court procedural record / Primary source

Bojana Vujić’s panel: plaintiff alone, Maja Petrović’s adoption-law defence and an agreed two-year framework — 6 February 2025

The plaintiff maintains his claim, acts without professional legal assistance and proposes “a settlement, which is very reasonable”. Maja Petrović defends the adoption under the 1976 Act while asking 30 days to obtain and study it; her statement gives both 1976 and 21 June 1979 for the ruling. On the parties’ joint proposal, the panel sets a two-year/12-hearing framework and schedules 26 March.

Basic Court in Novi Sad 2025-02-06
At the non-public hearing of 6 February 2025, P2.1435/24, Bojana Vujić presides, Milan Petrović attends personally and Maja Petrović represents the defendant CSR. The record states that the plaintiff’s former lawyer refused receipt of the summons after revocation of her power of attorney. The plaintiff tells the court he will act on his own and will not engage professional legal assistance. He maintains his action and claim. The plaintiff proposes examination of himself as a party and of the written documents, and offers the opposing party “a settlement, which is very reasonable”, requesting 30 days to reach it. No terms are recorded. He states that his ultimate aim is to establish paternity before the competent court in Kragujevac and explains his understanding that the full-adoption status under the 1976 Act prevents him from initiating that case before this one ends. Maja Petrović opposes the claim by invoking the Adoption Act of AP Vojvodina. In the same statement she places the adoption ruling in 1976 and on 21 June 1979, and requests 30 days to familiarize herself with the Act, which she must obtain from the Provincial Secretariat’s library. She accepts examination of the plaintiff and documents, opposes his proposed witnesses, and says she does not understand the proposed settlement. She also states that a social worker searched for his biological mother Cvita Miletić in 2005 and promises to provide the details. The plaintiff agrees to her requested period and adjournment. The record expressly says that both parties jointly propose a two-year procedural framework with 12 hearings and agree on the next date. The panel adopts that framework and schedules 26 March 2025 at 10:00. It grants the CSR representative 30 days for her position, the plaintiff 30 days for his witness decision and 60 days for employment or unemployment evidence. Fee exemption itself is not granted. The hearing ends at 09:25. A separately marked later author’s account states that his former lawyer refused to attend and that he does not know why she was summoned after representation ended. He describes his own settlement offer as unexpected for those present. That account is separate from the court record’s refusal-of-receipt entry.

Article 118 · Reading and correcting the hearing record

Civil Procedure Act of the Republic of Serbia

The form’s printed reference to “particulars under Article 118(1)” does not match the current Act’s numbering. Those particulars are in Article 116(1), to which the current small-claims provision, Article 474, expressly refers. The printed numbering corresponds to Articles 118(1) and 473 of the 2004 Act. This is evidence of an outdated reference in the form; it does not establish that the court applied the former Act or classified this identity/adoption dispute as a small claim.

…hours. They agree that the defendant’s representative should be granted the requested 30 days. The plaintiff is granted the requested **60 days** to submit evidence concerning his employment or unemployment.

Bojana Vujić’s hearing: “how he came under their jurisdiction”, CSR opposition and an order for adoption records — 26 March 2025
Why this source is linked
Related source

The March record preserves Maja Petrović’s renewed fifteen-day request for the statute and detailed position, and the plaintiff’s explanation of the previous witness deadline. The linked 6 February hearing records the earlier thirty-day periods for those different tasks. The March record also mentions both parties’ submissions dated 10 March; the reference to the defendant’s submission is preserved without inventing its unavailable contents.

It is recorded that the defendant’s submission dated **10.03.2025** is handed directly to the **plaintiff’s representative**. The plaintiff states that he maintains his action and the claim made, and his evidentiary proposal that the witness **Vinka Ignjatić** of Budisava, Petefi Šandora 168, and her son **Dragan Ignjatić** be examined in these proceedings. They were neighbours of the late adopters Gospava and Milorad Vasić and are acquainted with the fact that their marriage was dysfunctional even before the full adoption. He maintains his proposal to be examined as a party. He maintains his evidentiary proposal to inspect the Higher Court record already attached to the file, from the hearing in the divorce litigation of his adopters Gospava and Milorad. He maintains the proposal to request inspection of the file in which the marriage was dissolved, **P.1106/82**. He withdraws his evidentiary proposal to obtain officially from the Budisava Health Centre the medical record and medical history of his late adoptive mother Gospava Vasić. He proposes that the defendant be ordered to supply the documentation explaining how he **“came under their jurisdiction”**. These are his evidentiary proposals. He particularly emphasizes that, although at the previous hearing the court gave him the period he himself requested — 30 days to state whether he maintained the proposed witnesses or not — he did not properly understand what he was required to do. He therefore identified only Vinka Ignjatić of Budisava as a witness in his submission dated 10.03.2025, and at today’s hearing has stated that he also maintains the other proposed witnesses. The defendant’s representative states that she opposes the action and the claim in their entirety because the statutory conditions for annulment of the full adoption applicable when it was concluded are not fulfilled. She requests an additional period of **15 days**, during which I will provide a detailed written statement and supply the Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina containing the provisions. At today’s hearing I submit the social worker’s report in the search procedure for **Cvita Miletić**, dated **09.03.2005**. I oppose the evidentiary proposals to examine all the proposed witnesses about the dysfunctional marriage of the adopters Gospava and Milorad Vasić, since this is not a ground for annulment of the full adoption and such testimony by the proposed witnesses is questionable because of their indirect knowledge of the family circumstances. I agree that the plaintiff should be examined as a party and that the attached written documentation should be inspected, and I request 15 days to provide a detailed written statement and supply the then applicable Adoption Act of SAP Vojvodina which applied at the time of this plaintiff’s full adoption. She also opposes officially requesting inspection of the divorce litigation between the late adopters Gospava and Milorad Vasić, because it and the statements contained therein are not relevant to these proceedings for annulment of the full adoption, since the grounds for annulment must exist at the time the full adoption was established.
