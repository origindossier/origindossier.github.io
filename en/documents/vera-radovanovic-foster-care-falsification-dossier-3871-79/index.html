Vera Radovanović dossier 3871/79 — father when useful, unknown when identity is at stake

Confidential SIZ Novi Sad dossier for Miletić Milivoj. The document records a Pal/father trail, Cvita as visible mother, health insurance through the mother, and a healthy, cared-for child, while the formal father channel is left blank/unknown and the file moves toward adoption.

Social Protection SIZ in Novi Sad 1979-07-31
This is not a neutral foster-care file. It is the operative SIZ Novi Sad dossier in which Vera Radovanović records the facts needed to protect Milivoj’s origin, then cuts them out of the legal identity channel. The decisive collision is the father line. On 5 April 1979 the file says that the child was left without parental care because his natural father had died. On 8 April 1979 Vera writes that Pal Lakatoš raised the child and presented himself as the child’s natural father. In the same page she again writes that after the death of the natural father the child remained without parental care. On 16 April 1979 she places the child with Vizmeg Julka, the sister of father Pal, whom the child knew. This is the file’s own wording. Pal is not absent. Pal is present as the man who raised the child, as the man named in connection with natural fatherhood, and as father Pal through the foster placement. The same file then leaves the formal father field blank and lets the official reasoning speak of an unknown father. That is the mechanism: father when useful for placement and care, unknown when identity, paternity and inheritance must be protected. The mother line is attacked in the same way. Vera records severe moral allegations against Cvita after consultation with neighbors. In the same operational sequence she records that Milivoj was health insured through his mother, that the mother was registered with the Employment S.I.Z., and that the child was physically and mentally normally developed, healthy, well cared for, neat and later well nourished. The record therefore does not show a clean case of a nameless, abandoned and neglected child. It shows a child with a named mother, a Pal/father trail, a paternal-family placement, and a living identity reaction to the name Pal. The identity pressure is explicit. The child cried when he heard he was called Milivoj because they had called him Pal. Days later he stated that he was now called Mišo. On 21 June 1979 he was told that he would go home to mother and father, meaning the adopters. This is the pre-adoption identity-transfer layer before the civil-register replacement. Legal reading frame: Article 2, Article 5, Article 14, Article 15, Article 17, Article 30, Article 31, Article 41 and Article 42 of the SAP Vojvodina 1976 Adoption Act. These provisions matter because the file concerned origin, consent, child protection, professional assessment, rejection of unlawful adoption, full adoption, confidentiality and civil-register consequences. The articles are not the evidentiary center. The evidentiary center is the contradiction inside Vera’s own file: Pal/father trace present, formal father channel erased, child moved toward adoption.

Introduction before three written assessments; Vera evaluates, announces and consents

Milivoj was introduced to the prospective adopters on 5 June 1979, before the three written assessments by social worker Vera Radovanović on 12 June, pedagogue Milosava Gudelj on 18 June and psychologist Cvetko Ratković on 19 June. Vera was one of those three professional assessors: her report of 12 June expressly recommends approving the adoption. On 15 June, the same Vera informs the foster mother that Milivoj will be adopted at the end of June, before the other two written opinions; she is then appointed guardian on 20 June and gives consent to the adoption on 21 June, only 16 days after the introduction. Articles 14, 15 and 17 require examination of the statutory conditions and the child’s interests, professional opinions and a finding that the conditions are met before the adoption hearing is scheduled.

Revision of 29 May 2026: the appellate judgment confronted with the 1976 law and the identity evidence
Why this source is linked
Related source

The linked dossier supplies the historical record for the revision’s allegation that Vera Radovanović combined narrative-building, recommendations, guardianship and consent. Her recorded acts and the author’s conflict-of-interest conclusion are distinguished.

The files show that Vera Radovanović:

  • participated in shaping the narrative about the plaintiff’s mother;
  • participated in relativising Pavle Lakatoš’s paternal line;
  • maintained and prepared the case documentation;
  • recommended the particular adopters specifically for the minor Milivoj Miletić;
  • was appointed ad hoc guardian to consent to full adoption;
  • consented to full adoption;
  • then reappeared in 1984 in a new cycle of institutional removal and a new adoption of the same child.

In his appeal, the plaintiff clearly explained why assessing this concentration of roles in one person is decisive: the same official could not simultaneously:

  • build the case;
  • create the factual narrative;
  • recommend particular adopters;
  • assume the role of protector of the child’s interests;
  • and then consent to the outcome she herself had previously prepared, which proved wrong and contrary to the child’s best interests.

The Court of Appeal did not assess these arguments, did not say that they were irrelevant to a correct and lawful decision, and did not consider them in any way. It completely disregarded them. It did not call the argument immaterial. It did not reject it.

Such failure to assess the grounds of appeal violates Article 396(1) CPA and renders the second-instance judgment unlawful.

6. The second-instance court did not genuinely answer the ground of appeal concerning the guardianship authority’s competence

The plaintiff’s appeal did not challenge in the abstract that the Self-Managing Community of Interest for Social Protection in Novi Sad could have had certain powers as a guardianship authority under a general decision from 1977.

The plaintiff challenged:

  • by which specific act that authority acquired competence over the minor Milivoj Miletić before 21 June 1979;
  • when that competence was established;
  • on what facts;
  • how this fits with the Municipal Court in Novi Sad’s letter of 20 July 1979 directing the Secretariat for Veterans’ and Disability Affairs and Social Protection, as guardianship authority, to appoint a guardian to protect the rights and interests of the minor Milivoj Miletić in the probate proceedings after Pavle Lakatoš’s death.

Appeal of 18 March 2026: nullity, unanswered evidence and the second adoption
Why this source is linked
Related source

The appeal questions Vera Radovanović’s monitoring and case narrative, especially value judgments about Cvita based on neighbour consultation, alongside entries that describe a cared-for child. The relation opens the actual monitoring file for comparison.

The court was required to explain how a new adoption in 1984 was possible if a valid full adoption already existed in 1979, one that could not be dissolved under the law then in force. By failing to do so, it failed to apply substantive law to a decisive fact that directly undermines the conclusion that the first adoption was lawful.

4. Wrongly reducing the dispute to a mere family-law framework

The court treated the case as a typical family dispute, although it is a dispute against the guardianship body concerning the lawfulness of its historical and present conduct, identity, origins, inheritance status and the consequences of an administrative change of identity.

The first-instance court thereby wrongly marginalised the identity dimension of the dispute, although precisely that dimension determines a proper understanding of the legal significance of the contested adoption and the subsequent institutional maintenance of its consequences.

VI. INCORRECT ASSESSMENT OF EVIDENCE

The first-instance court did not critically assess the contents of the archive documentation. Instead, it effectively equated its formal authenticity and official provenance with the truth of its contents and the lawfulness of the conduct of the body that created it.

It accepted particularly uncritically the documents created by the very body whose conduct is contested.

Nor did the first-instance court critically assess the fact that the defendant’s internal documentation contains a degrading narrative permeated with value judgments and sex-based and cultural prejudices about my biological mother. Instead, it effectively accepted that content without genuine legal and evidentiary scrutiny. It thereby did more than uncritically adopt the defendant’s internal documentation: it indirectly provided a legitimising framework for descriptions contaminated by value judgments that could not be treated as a neutral and reliable basis for judicial conclusions.

The contradictions and problems concerning the following were not properly assessed:

  • the monitoring sheet of contacts and casework;
  • social worker Vera Radovanović’s report;
  • formulaic and mutually inconsistent professional assessments;
  • moral characterisations of the mother based on “consultation with neighbours”, without a serious evidentiary basis;
  • the fact that CSR’s own internal documentation confirms in several places that the child was well cared for, tidy, staying with paternal relatives and undergoing accelerated psychological preparation for an identity change.

In other words, the court did not compare and weigh conflicting evidence, but privileged the defendant’s internal documentation in advance.
