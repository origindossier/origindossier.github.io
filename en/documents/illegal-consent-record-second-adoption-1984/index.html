Milorad Vasić consent - false parental position from the 1979 void adoption record

Milorad Vasić gives consent as “father” of Vasić Milan. That position is not biological or neutral; it derives from the contested 1979 full-adoption record that replaced Milivoj Miletić with Milan Vasić. The 1984 consent therefore becomes the operational bridge from the first identity cut to the second adoption.

Novi Sad Social Work Centre 1984-09-12
Milorad Vasić does not appear as a biological father of Milivoj Miletić. He appears as the administrative father of Vasić Milan, a position produced by the contested 1979 full-adoption record. The phrase “my minor son Vasić Milan” is therefore not a neutral parental statement. It is the use of a status generated by the first adoption to authorize a second adoption. This document is the clean double-adoption bridge. In 1979 the child enters the adoption procedure as Miletić Milivoj and is administratively rewritten as Vasić Milan. In 1984, Milorad Vasić uses that generated parental position to consent to a new adoption. CSR Novi Sad receives that consent as a valid parental input instead of reopening the original identity, the biological line, Cvita Miletić, Pavle / Pál Lakatoš, and the probate trace. Vera Radovanović is again on the chain. In June 1979 she signs the Vasić suitability report and acts as special guardian in the full-adoption proceeding. On 12 September 1984 she hears Milorad Vasić’s consent for the second adoption. The repeated actor is not incidental. It is the same functional channel: first the parental void and adoption identity are produced; then the consent derived from that production is accepted as input for the next identity cut.

  • effectively presents the first adoptive parent, Vasić Milorad, as the holder of parental rights who “gives consent” to a new adoption;

  • violent manipulation of the child’s identity continues: a child already erased as Palika Lakatoš and renamed Vasić Milan is now further “transferred” into another family, with a newly altered line of belonging;
  • the guardianship body effectively behaves as though it owns the child and his identity: first name, surname, family status and affiliation are changed, while the first adoptive parent is presented as a “father” whenever needed merely to satisfy the condition of “parental consent” on paper.

When the guardianship body:

  • holds the negative assessment from the psychologist of the Centre for Social Work in Kragujevac, stating that adoption is not recommended;
  • knows that full adoption from 1979 already exists;
  • nevertheless organizes the first adoptive parent’s “consent” as though he were the biological father, to create an appearance of legality before the second family,

this is no longer an ordinary “omission”, but:

  • a conscious and continuing violation of the law;
  • involving deception of the second adoptive parents too;
  • and further continuation of the forced assimilation of a child whose identity changes to suit the case file, rather than in accordance with the law and his rights.

I therefore propose that the Court:

  1. clearly establish that double adoption in 1979 and 1984 is contrary to Article 7 of the 1976 Adoption Act of the Socialist Autonomous Province of Vojvodina;
  2. establish that disregard of the negative psychological assessment from 1984 and manufacture of the first adoptive parent’s “consent” as a “father” prove that the defendant acted not as a body protecting a child, but as a body disposing of a child and his identity;
  3. treat all of this as evidence of the continuity of unlawful conduct, rather than isolated technical errors.
