Origin Dossier — Executive Summary

This is not an adoption story

This dossier is not a private family dispute, a sentimental childhood story, or a neutral adoption-history file. It documents a chain of state, court, civil-register and social-welfare acts by which a child born as Milivoj Miletić was removed from his original identity, his paternal line, his maternal line, his language trace and his inheritance position.

The child was born on 28 June 1974 in Novi Sad. His mother was Cvita Miletić, recorded through the Croatian maternal line. His father was Pavle / Pal Lakatoš, the Hungarian paternal line preserved through court, family and probate traces. The case is not about a harmless change of name. It is about administrative identity substitution and forced assimilation through records.

Evidence to examine first

The original birth-registration form of 28 June 1974 names Cvita Miletić as the mother and expressly records her ethnicity as Croatian. This is the direct civil-register source for the maternal origin described in the overview.

Cvita Miletić and the maternal ethnicity field

In court file P.1642/76, Cvita testified on 18 January 1977 that she had a son, Milivoj, with the defendant. The hearing record of 26 October 1977 identifies Lakatos Pál as Hungarian and records his own statement that he had a son with Cvita.

Cvita’s testimony, 18 January 1977 · Pál’s recorded ethnicity · Pál’s own statement

The core chain is blunt:

Milivoj Miletić → Vasić Milan → Petrović Milan

That is not a biography. It is a documentary trail of identity cutting.

Core charge of the dossier

The documentary record shows heavy illegality, not a sequence of technical errors. The child was treated as administrative material: moved from his original civil identity into a Serbian adoptive record, pushed out of the paternal inheritance track, then moved again through a second adoption loop.

The dossier documents:

  1. the original civil identity of Milivoj Miletić;
  2. the Croatian maternal line through Cvita Miletić;
  3. the Hungarian paternal line through Pavle / Pal Lakatoš;
  4. a probate track in which the child first appears as son and heir;
  5. a full-adoption route in 1979 that produces the Vasić record;
  6. the use of that Vasić record to neutralize origin, fatherhood and inheritance;
  7. the collapse of the Vasić household after the child was placed there;
  8. a second adoption in 1984, fed by the first contested adoptive record;
  9. a new civil-register layer as Petrović Milan;
  10. the present-day defense by CSR Novi Sad that the whole chain was allegedly lawful.

This is why the dossier treats the violation as continuing. The injury did not end in 1979. It continued through 1984, through later civil-register layers, through the inheritance exclusion, and through the present institutional insistence that the record was lawful.

Forced administrative assimilation

The forced-assimilation axis is not decorative. It is central.

A child of a Croatian mother and a Hungarian father was moved into a Serbian adoptive administrative framework. The Vasić record did not protect origin. It covered origin. The later Petrović layer did not repair the first substitution. It reused it.

The mechanism is visible:

Croatian mother + Hungarian father → Serbian Vasić adoptive record → second Petrović record

This is administrative Serbization through civil-register power: the original identity is replaced, the father is suppressed, the mother is morally disqualified, the Hungarian language trace is broken, and the child is presented to later files through the substitute adoptive identity.

The dossier does not claim that every document uses the word “assimilation.” It shows the mechanism in the papers: origin erased, name replaced, father bypassed, inheritance narrowed out, and the substitute Serbian adoptive record used as legal reality.

The 1979 core: identity, fatherhood, inheritance

The 1979 node is the center of the case.

In the probate file O.196/79, the child appears as Miletić Milivoj, born 28 June 1974, connected to the estate of Pavle / Pal Lakatoš. The court requires a guardian to protect the rights and interests of the minor in the probate procedure. The file first opens a paternal and inheritance trace.

At the same time, the social-welfare route moves the child toward full adoption. The same child exists in one channel as a potential son and heir, while in another channel he is processed as a child to be fully transferred into another family. This is not normal procedure. It is a collision between inheritance protection and identity disposal.

The key contradiction is simple:

  • in the probate track, Milivoj is treated as a child whose rights in the paternal estate must be protected;
  • in the adoption track, the same child is prepared for full identity transfer into the Vasić record.

The state record therefore splits the child in two: the living child and his inheritance-bearing identity.

Vera Radovanović and the engineered record

The social-welfare file is not neutral. It records the method.

The tracking sheet for Miletić Milivoj, handled by Vera Radovanović, contains the narrative used to remove the child from the original family line. The mother is described through degrading sexual and moral labels without judicial proof. At the same time, the file records that the child was physically and psychologically normally developed, well cared for, clean and without behavioral problems.

That contradiction matters. A child cannot at the same time be proof of total maternal neglect and also be well cared for inside the family environment. The file therefore does not simply describe danger. It builds a case for removal.

The same record also captures the identity-conditioning process. The child cried when he heard he was called Milivoj because he had been called Pal / Palika, connected to the paternal line. Instead of protecting that identity signal, the institution redirected the child toward a new name and then toward the Vasić adoption.

Vera Radovanović is not a peripheral name. The same official line appears across the chain: narrative construction, choice of adopters, special-guardian consent, the 1979 full adoption, and later the 1984 consent record that turns the first adopter into a false parental source for the second adoption.

Misogyny and chauvinism in the file

The dossier treats the language about the mother as evidence, not as style.

Cvita Miletić, recorded as Croatian, is not processed as a subject whose position must be heard and legally assessed. She is processed through accusations of prostitution, alcohol, vagrancy and moral failure. The file does not present court judgments, police files or medical proof for that level of destruction. It uses the old administrative weapon: degrade the mother, then remove the child.

This is not professional social work. It is a misogynistic and chauvinistic record used against a Croatian mother and against a Hungarian-Croatian child. Poverty and instability are not treated as reasons to assist the family; they are converted into a moral indictment used to cut the child out of his origin.

The “king” document: the child was not abandoned in the way CSR suggests

The probate record of 19 March 1979 is a direct blow to the later social-welfare narrative. It records that the minor Milivoj was at that time with the grandparents / parents of the deceased. That means the child was not simply on the street, not floating in social emptiness, and not without a family line capable of being identified.

It also records that the deceased father had recognized the child. This does not close every legal question by itself, but it destroys the later bureaucratic fiction that the paternal line could be treated as empty or irrelevant.

This is why the dossier reads the 1979 social-welfare narrative as a self-incriminating record. The institution did not merely fail to see the paternal family. It processed the case in a way that bypassed it.

The Vasić layer: substitute identity and failed placement

The 1979 full adoption creates the Vasić civil-register layer. The child enters the procedure as Miletić Milivoj and is moved into the record as Vasić Milan.

That layer later collapses in the divorce file P.1106/82. The court says the Vasić marriage had been harmonious for eighteen years and that disagreements began after the adoption of the child. In effect, the court records the failure of the first placement while using sterile language that shifts the explanatory burden onto the child.

The same divorce material contains a deeper documentary contamination: the child is in some places described as adopted, and in other places as if born in the marriage. This shows how the 1979 adoptive record began to manufacture legal reality beyond the adoption file itself.

The file does not name the violence and domestic collapse that would have forced a harder question: into what kind of house was the child placed by the rushed 1979 procedure?

The 1984 loop: false consent and forbidden second adoption

The 1984 adoption file is structural proof.

A full adoption had already been created in 1979. Yet in 1984 the same child is moved toward another adoption. Milorad Vasić appears in the record as if he were a parental source giving consent for the adoption of “his son” Vasić Milan. That status was not biological fatherhood. It was an administrative position produced by the contested 1979 record.

The second adoption therefore uses the first substitution as input. That is the double adoption loop.

The file also contains a negative psychological recommendation concerning the Petrović couple: adoption should not be accepted; foster care is suggested instead. The procedure continues. The child is not treated as a rights-bearing person whose trauma and identity must be repaired. He is again processed as an object to be placed.

Current responsibility

The present case is not only about Vera Radovanović in 1979. It is also about today’s CSR Novi Sad and the present court record.

When CSR Novi Sad now argues that everything was lawful, it does not merely defend an old file. It re-signs the record. It adopts the misogynistic attack on the Croatian mother, the suppression of the Hungarian paternal line, the erasure of inheritance position, and the second adoption loop as if they were legitimate social-welfare work.

That is why the dossier treats the violation as continuing. Every present defense of the falsified chain is a new institutional act sustaining the original injury.

Why the case matters outside one courtroom

This case concerns identity, parentage, language, inheritance, child protection, minority rights, abuse of adoption, and state responsibility for records that manufacture false identity. It also concerns the question whether a present-day court will confront the papers or protect the reputation of institutions.

The judgment will not be read only in Novi Sad. It will be translated, archived and read by lawyers, researchers, minority-rights observers, adoption-abuse networks and institutions outside Serbia. The question is not whether Serbia will be offended. The question is whether Serbia’s institutions will continue to defend an identity substitution file after the contradiction has been documented.
