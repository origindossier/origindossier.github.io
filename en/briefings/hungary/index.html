Hungarian family connection, language and the break in paternal identity

Lakatos Pál’s divorce file records his son born to Cvita Miletić; later welfare and adoption records describe an unknown father. For Hungarian institutions, this opens a documentary question about family continuity and paternal identity. The court record expressly identifies the father as Hungarian. The 1979 probate proceedings initially include Milivoj in his estate; subsequent proceedings exclude him from heirship. Meanwhile, the adoption act directs the Vasić name and new parental entries. Compare these sequences with the author’s challenge, distinguishing recorded family statements, formal paternity and later institutional conclusions.

Evidence to examine first

In the divorce record Lakatoš Pavle is expressly identified as Hungarian; Cvita’s own testimony says she bore their son Milivoj in 1974. The 16 April 1979 welfare decision later calls the father unknown. Compare these specific entries while keeping the recorded family statements distinct from the contested question of formal legal paternity.

Father’s recorded ethnicity · Cvita’s testimony about their son · 1979: father recorded as unknown

Vera’s note of 16 April 1979 records that the child cried on hearing the name Milivoj because he had been called Pal, and that the foster mother was told to use his name. The note of 19 April records that he now said his name was Mišo. This provides a contemporaneous name and paternal-identity trace before the later Vasić entry.

Note dated 16 April 1979 · Pal / Milivoj: the child’s reaction · 19 April 1979: Mišo

Exclusion from paternal inheritance using an answer about adoption

The 23 November 1979 probate ruling lists Milivoj as Pavle’s son and awards him one quarter of the estate. Pervaz’s appeal expressly contests that share. In the later proceedings, the welfare authority replies only that Pavle had not adopted Milivoj, while the 10 November 1980 ruling treats the same reply as establishing that he was not Pavle’s son either. Follow the initial inheritance entry and the narrower reply against the later exclusion: this is the specific break in the documented paternal inheritance trail.

1979: Milivoj listed as son · 1979: one-quarter inheritance share · Pervaz contests the child’s share · 20 October: not adopted by Pavle · 10 November: not the deceased’s son
