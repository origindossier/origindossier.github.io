The child’s voice, continuity of care and access to origins across a lifetime.

A 1979 casework file records the child crying over his name and contains family traces alongside descriptions used to justify his removal. The adult’s claim recounts violence and abandonment in the first adoptive household, while the adopter’s own 1984 statement says he placed the child with a foster family to preserve his new marriage and had no contact with him afterwards.

Evidence to examine first

Introduction before three written assessments; Vera evaluates, announces and consents

Milivoj was introduced to the prospective adopters on 5 June 1979, before the three written assessments by social worker Vera Radovanović on 12 June, pedagogue Milosava Gudelj on 18 June and psychologist Cvetko Ratković on 19 June. Vera was one of those three professional assessors: her report of 12 June expressly recommends approving the adoption. On 15 June, the same Vera informs the foster mother that Milivoj will be adopted at the end of June, before the other two written opinions; she is then appointed guardian on 20 June and gives consent to the adoption on 21 June, only 16 days after the introduction. Articles 14, 15 and 17 require examination of the statutory conditions and the child’s interests, professional opinions and a finding that the conditions are met before the adoption hearing is scheduled.

5 June: introduction to the adopters · 12 June: Vera recommends approval · 15 June: Vera announces adoption · 18 June: Gudelj’s favourable written opinion · 19 June: Ratković’s favourable written opinion · 20 June: Vera appointed guardian and gives consent in the 21 June record · Article 14: preparatory examination · Article 15: professional opinions · Article 17: conditions before scheduling

The 1982 Vasić divorce decision records that Milorad had not paid support for Milan since May and gives that as a reason for an interim support measure. His own 1984 statement later records placement with a foster family in August 1983 and no contact since then. These dated records add contemporaneous support and contact evidence alongside the author’s account of childhood violence.

1982: unpaid support and interim measure · 1984 statement: no contact after placement

The psychologist assessing the Petrović couple says their adoption request should not be accepted and recommends foster care instead. The 26 September 1984 adoption record nevertheless states that a positive opinion was given and the statutory conditions were fulfilled. Read the two documents together to examine how the professional warning was handled before another placement.

Questions to examine

  1. How were the child’s recorded voice and identifiable family relationships weighed in decisions about care and identity?
  2. What continuity of protection existed between the first placement, the end of contact and the second adoption?
  3. How can the adult examine and challenge records governing his origins after decades of identity substitutions?
