Contemporary reasoning, institutional accountability and the practical examination of evidence.

The 2026 first-instance judgment accepts written documents because authorised and qualified persons produced them, and credits the claimant’s account where it agrees with that evidence. The appeal and revision ask whether this method answers the actual contradictions about parental origin, statutory conditions and a second adoption after full adoption.

Evidence to examine first

The first-instance judgment accepts the written documentation because its authors were authorised and qualified, accepts the claimant’s testimony where it agrees with the credited evidence, and rejects hearing Vinka Ignjatić and Dragan Ignjatić because the facts were already sufficiently examined and further evidence would prolong the case. These are specific judicial reasons against which to examine access to an effective consideration of the evidence.

Authority and qualifications of authors · Accepted parts of the testimony · Reasons for rejecting witnesses

The appellate judgment answers the objection about the two different parental-condition routes by saying the conditions were met “in any event”. Read that passage beside Article 30(1) and (4), which state different conditions, and the revision’s maintained objection. This makes the quality of the court’s answer directly examinable.

Appellate judgment: conditions met “in any event” · Article 30(1): parental conditions · Article 30(4): the other parent’s consent

The cited birth extract names a different parental pair

The first-instance judgment says it established the 1984 adoption by Periša and Georgina Petrović from birth-register extract 2463/1979. That extract names the child Petrović Milan but retains Milorad and Gospava Vasić in the parent fields. The Petrović couple is identified in the separate 1984 adoption record. Read the three documents together to check the mismatch between the fact stated and the particular document cited as its proof.

2026: the court’s evidentiary explanation · Birth extract: number 2463/1979 · Extract: Petrović child name · Extract: Vasić parent entries · 1984 record: the Petrović adopters

Questions to examine

  1. Does the reasoning separately establish the facts and legal conditions, rather than rely on the authority of the issuing institution?
  2. Can the reader trace an explicit answer to the mother’s prior consent and the Article 40 transition?
  3. How are an individual’s documentary objections examined when he has represented himself during substantial parts of the proceedings?
