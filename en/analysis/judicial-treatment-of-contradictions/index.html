Analysis / Judicial method

Judicial Treatment of Documentary Contradictions

The present analytical question is not only what happened historically, but how the current court reacts once the contradiction is fully presented.

Selected 2026-03-18

This article isolates recurring reasoning patterns: archival existence treated as legality, missing engagement with conflict points, and the recycling of institutional narrative in place of review.

Documentary continuation — the judicial responses in 2026

The Basic Court judgment dated 9 February 2026 rejects the claim for annulment of the full adoption as absolutely void. Its opening quotation of the requested relief is not the judgment’s operative outcome. On page 6, the court accepts the written documents because authorised and qualified persons produced them; it accepts the plaintiff’s testimony in the relevant parts consistent with the evidence it credited. It refuses to hear Vinka and Dragan Ignjatić, considering the facts sufficiently examined without them. These passages make the evidentiary method itself available for scrutiny.

The witness proposal was already present in the 2024 statement of claim and retained in the record of 26 March 2025. It must be distinguished from the final hearing record of 9 February 2026, whose two accounts of reading the file and documents the author disputes in a separately labelled statement. His objection concerns what happened at that final hearing, rather than an assertion that the earlier witness proposal never existed.

Compare the decision with the grounds actually advanced

The appeal of 18 March 2026 challenges the evidence assessment, parental origin, statutory conditions and the unresolved second-adoption transition, including Articles 7 and 40. The Appellate Court judgment of 22 April 2026 rejects the appeal and affirms the first-instance judgment. It prints Article 31 while describing the conditions in Article 30; the full translation preserves this source discrepancy and identifies it separately.

The revision dated 29 May 2026 then develops the challenge to both judgments, including Article 40, identity, forced assimilation, institutional responsibility and the treatment of the evidence. It is a request to the Supreme Court, not a Supreme Court decision. The final submission of 12 January 2026 supplies the earlier written argument against which these judicial responses can be read.
