### Author’s message — original English section

Dear Prime Minister Magyar, 

I am writing to remind you of my previous email sent to peter.magyar@europarl.europa.eu regarding my documented case from Délvidék / Vojvodina, involving the forced assimilation and administrative erasure of a child of Hungarian paternal origin through two illegal adoptions in Serbia in 1979 and 1984. 

Since that message, there has been a further and serious development. 

The courts of Serbia have now, through both a first-instance and a second-instance decision, maintained the legal consequences of the 1979 adoption. In substance, Serbia’s judiciary has not corrected the original severance of my Hungarian paternal line. It has confirmed it again. 

This means that Hungary’s southern neighbour has, through its courts, performed a renewed legal subjugation of my identity: it has continued to deny my original name, my paternal Hungarian line, my separation from the Hungarian language, and my displaced inheritance position. 

For clarity: the identity confusion surrounding my name is not something I created for myself. It was created by the quasi-state of Serbia through its social welfare authorities, adoption procedures, civil registry interventions, and later court decisions. 

I was born as Milivoj Miletić. My biological father was Pál Lakatoš. In my early childhood within the paternal environment, I was known as Palika Lakatoš. The later administrative identities were imposed through state procedures, not freely chosen by me. 

The case is not abstract. It is documented through birth records, adoption records, social welfare files, probate proceedings, court decisions, DNA evidence, and testimony from the biological family. 

I also previously addressed SVM/VMSZ. I informed them of the documented facts and requested their official position. They did not respond. 

This silence is politically relevant. SVM/VMSZ presents itself as the representative of Hungarians in Serbia and receives political and financial support for that role. Yet in a concrete case where a Hungarian paternal line, language, identity, and inheritance position were administratively cut from a child, there was no response.
