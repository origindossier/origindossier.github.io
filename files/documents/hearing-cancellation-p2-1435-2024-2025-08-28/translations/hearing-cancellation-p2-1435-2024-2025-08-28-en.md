## Cancellation of the 3 September 2025 hearing: legal analysis

**Editorial legal analysis prepared with AI assistance.** This is separate from the two source documents and the author's account; it is not a final judicial finding. The analysis applies the Serbian Code of Civil Procedure (ZPP) in force on 3 September 2025 and uses official court and legislative sources.

### The concrete procedural problem

The supplied order in P2.1435/24, dated **28 August 2025**, cancels the hearing of **3 September at 10:00** and sets **15 October at 10:30**, courtroom 210/2: **42 additional calendar days**. Its introductory text names judge **Bojana Vujić**. The CSR request, dated and stamped as received **27 August**, is signed by director **Katarina Stolić**. It asserts illness of the official representing the Centre and commitments of other representatives in other cases.

Neither supplied page shows an assessment of the actual inability to attend, the competing commitments or the feasibility of substitution. The request does not identify the ill official, specify the period of incapacity, identify the competing cases or list supporting attachments. This is a concrete weakness of the justification shown to the reader. It does not establish that no other material exists in the court file.

The author states that he was **only informed of the cancellation at court on 3 September**; the order was not handed to him there. A postal carrier delivered the order to his home on **4 September 2025**, one day after the scheduled hearing. The supplied envelope visibly bears a **2 September 2025 postal postmark** and identifies the Basic Court in Novi Sad, P2.1435/24 and judge VUJIĆ. Its delivery-notice form has no completed attempted-delivery date, time or deliverer's signature. The scan corroborates the postal mark, not the author's reported **4 September delivery** or the exact history of dispatch from the court. The court order predates his reported agreement by six days: his later assent cannot explain why the court made its earlier decision.

### 1. Absence does not automatically require adjournment

**Article 108(1) ZPP** makes adjournment exceptional, expressly addressing the taking of evidence or the judge's inability to attend. **Article 311(1)** provides for continuing the main hearing with the attending party when the other, duly summoned party is absent. **Article 310** requires checking summons and justification for absence when opening the hearing. [Official ZPP, articles 108 and 310–311](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/Закон%20о%20парничном%20поступку%2010-23.pdf).

**Application:** the CSR's wish to have its particular representative present is not itself an automatic right to stop the hearing. The request needed a concrete assessment of why the hearing could not proceed, including substitution and any evidentiary need. Calling the change a cancellation does not answer that question. These two pages do not disclose such an assessment.

### 2. Establishing inability to attend due to illness

The cited ZPP provisions do **not prescribe a universal rule that every request must include a medical certificate**. The precise issue is whether the reason establishes inability to attend at the relevant time and whether a practical alternative exists. For a later application for restoration after losing a procedural right, **article 113(2)** expressly addresses appropriate supporting or proposed evidence; that is a different procedural mechanism from an advance adjournment request. [Official ZPP, articles 109–113](https://www.va.vi.sud.rs/files/zakon_o_parnicnom_postupku.pdf).

The following decisions concern justification of absence and presumed withdrawal under article 311(2), rather than a general entitlement to an advance adjournment. They explain why both illness and available substitutes matter:

- **Prev 206/2025, 17 April 2025:** the Supreme Court corrected a presumed withdrawal of the claim where the sole remaining attorney's illness justified absence; the alleged second attorney's authority had already been revoked. It does not establish an unconditional entitlement to adjourn every hearing. [Official decision](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sr-lat/node/93286).
- **Rev 336/2021, 14 July 2021:** planned leave did not justify absence where there was time to arrange substitution and another authorised attorney also failed to attend without justification. [Official decision](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sr-lat/rev-3362021-3191245-%C5%BEalba-protiv-re%C5%A1enja).
- **Rev2 3119/2021, 20 October 2022:** a medical report did not settle the issue where other authorised representatives existed and a real possibility of attendance was found. [Official decision](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sr-lat/rev2-31192021-3191253-revizija-protiv-re%C5%A1enja).

**Application:** director Katarina Stolić's assertion cannot be treated as a medical finding. Equally, absence of a certificate on these pages does not prove fabrication. The demonstrable criticism is the lack of a visible, specific evaluation connecting illness and other commitments to inability of the **Centre as a party** to participate on 3 September.

### 3. The Centre has several lawful routes to representation

**Articles 77, 85(4), 89(1)(3) and 89(2)** distinguish the legal person's registered representative, an authorised employed law graduate who has passed the judicial examination (pravosudni ispit), and an attorney. Transfer by a non-attorney representative needs express authority. Any replacement must therefore be legally qualified and properly authorised. [Official ZPP, representation provisions](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/Закон%20о%20парничном%20поступку%2010-23.pdf).

Judge **Jelena Borovac's** commentary in the Supreme Court of Cassation's **Bulletin 3/2012, printed page 33**, specifically discusses replacement by another representative, the legal person's own representative or an engaged attorney. This is published judicial commentary, not a binding judgment. [Official Bulletin](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/files/Bilteni/VrhovniKasacioniSud/bilten_2012-3.pdf#page=33).

**Application:** the request itself acknowledges other representatives but supplies no concrete timetable conflict. Their being busy cannot simply substitute for evaluating whether any authorised person could attend.

### 4. Delivery matters independently of the adjournment reason

**Article 364(1)** governs service of orders made outside a hearing; **article 106** permits electronic cancellation notices with a return confirmation of receipt. [Official ZPP, articles 106 and 364](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/Закон%20о%20парничном%20поступку%2010-23.pdf).

**Application:** the author's account raises the concrete issue of whether effective notice reached the plaintiff before his planned appearance. The reported written delivery on 4 September occurred after that appearance and one day after the canceled hearing date. The visible postal mark is five days after the order and only one day before the scheduled hearing. It does not alone establish the court's dispatch date, actual delivery or when the plaintiff learned of the cancellation. The supplied blank delivery-notice fields do not establish any attempted-delivery time. The relevant additional material is the service record, court dispatch record and any confirmed electronic notice. The 27 August stamp on PDF page 2 proves receipt of the **CSR request by the court**, not service of the **court's order on the plaintiff**. His reported unnecessary journey remains a concrete consequence; the available material does not establish that the court sent nothing.

### 5. Reasons and legal remedies

**Article 329(2)** excludes an appeal against adjournment decisions. **Article 365(1)–(2)** requires reasons when a separate appeal is permitted. [Official ZPP, articles 329 and 365](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/Закон%20о%20парничном%20поступку%2010-23.pdf).

**Application:** the missing reasons and appeal instructions do not, by themselves, establish illegality of this case-management order. The stronger objection concerns the substantive basis, the evaluation of substitution and timely communication. A non-appealable order still does not confer an unrestricted power to postpone.

### 6. Legal inexperience and later agreement

**Article 85(10)** provides the narrower warning about engaging a representative for an unrepresented party who does not exercise rights through ignorance. **Article 313** addresses factual clarification. **Articles 2 and 5** protect equal treatment and an opportunity to respond. [Official ZPP, relevant provisions](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/Закон%20о%20парничном%20поступку%2010-23.pdf).

**Application:** the court must apply statutory warnings and ensure equal treatment. The scope of article 85's warning does not amount to general legal advice. His ignorance cannot replace the court's own examination of a request made by an institution with professional representation. His reported agreement to a date is not automatically an admission that every asserted reason is true, or a waiver of every procedural guarantee. It cannot retroactively establish the reasons for the decision of 28 August. This does not presume a rule requiring a prior hearing of the plaintiff before every scheduling change.

### 7. The added 42 days require scrutiny

**Article 108(2)–(3)** requires a new procedural timeframe and limits its duration to no more than one third of the originally set timeframe, while requiring the next hearing's place and date to be set immediately. The supplied order sets a new date and place. The [archived record of 6 February 2025](/en/documents/hearing-record-p2-1435-2024-2025-02-06), source page 3, already sets a **two-year timeframe and 12 hearings**. The 28 August order does not expressly state a separate new timeframe. The 42-day interval between hearing dates is not itself the overall procedural timeframe; respect for article 108(2) must be assessed against the actual timeframe orders, rather than inferred from that interval alone. [Official ZPP, article 108](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/Закон%20о%20парничном%20поступку%2010-23.pdf).

The significance of this identity and adoption dispute to the plaintiff, the prior course of hearings, the reasons for delay and the parties' conduct all matter when evaluating reasonable time. **Article 4 of the Law on Protection of the Right to Trial within a Reasonable Time** requires an overall assessment, rather than an automatic threshold of a particular number of days. Its 2023 amendments did not change that article. [Official original law, article 4](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/Zakon%20o%20za%C5%A1titi%20prava%20na%20su%C4%91enje%20u%20razumnom%20roku.pdf#page=2); [enacted 2023 amendments](https://www.parlament.gov.rs/upload/archive/files/cir/pdf/zakoni/13_saziv/1839-23.pdf).

**Assessment:** the shown request is generic while its effect is specific: another **42 days** without the planned hearing. No visible assessment explains why substitution or proceeding with the plaintiff was insufficient. Combined with the author's account of learning of the cancellation at court on 3 September and postal delivery at home on 4 September, this creates a serious, verifiable concern about timely notification, procedural diligence and equality. Those records are needed to assess the justification for adjournment and the timeliness of notification.
