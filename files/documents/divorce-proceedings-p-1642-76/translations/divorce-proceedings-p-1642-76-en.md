For the plaintiff: in person; Lukač Agošton, attorney from Temerin.

Counsel for the plaintiff withdraws the claim concerning Lakatoš Irena, as she has reached adulthood and will pursue her rights to maintenance in separate proceedings. Counsel reduces the claim concerning Lakatoš Ištvan and requests 600 dinars per month, calculated from the date the action was brought and for as long as the statutory conditions exist.

The defendant does not oppose the claim as formulated. As regards fault, he asks the court to establish the fault of both parties, since the plaintiff is also at fault for the divorce.

Regarding evidence that the plaintiff is at fault for the divorce, he requests a period of 15 days in which to provide evidence to the court.

The court issues the following **ORDER**.

### PDF page 2 — Witness statements

The witnesses shall be heard.

#### Witness MOJSINOV SPASA

Daughter of Jakšić Damjan, born in 1916 in Mol, from Novi Sad, Teodora Pavlovića Street no. 34, unrelated to the parties, having been warned, states:

I have known the parties for 12 years. I know that they lived badly together. The defendant mainly stayed on the farmstead; in the last five years he was separated from the family and came only occasionally. The plaintiff went to him and asked him to return home and not live on the farmstead because the conditions there were poor. Four years ago, the defendant began living with Miletić Cveta and had a child with her. I heard that he also lived with a woman called Julka and that she had an abortion; that is what Julka told me.

The defendant told me that he had quarreled with the plaintiff because she had not paid him for the milk he brought from the farmstead. Since then he had completely separated from the family. As far as I know, the plaintiff treated the defendant well as a wife. I know that she gave him money, helped with work on the farmstead and called him home.

She lent him the money.

The defendant denies having had relations with Julka. He states that this is an invention of the witness, with whom he is in a dispute because he had a court case against her.

The witness does not claim expenses.

#### Witness DIMIĆ ERŽEBET

Daughter of Cegledi Peter, born in 1908 in Belo Blato, unrelated to the parties, having been warned, from Novi Sad, Koste Racina Street no. 19, states:

I worked as a day laborer for the defendant. On the farmstead I saw Miletić Cvita, who lived with the defendant outside marriage and had a child with him, Milivoj, in 1974.

I know that the defendant drank. The plaintiff is very hardworking and worked a great deal to acquire property. I know this because I have known them for about 20 years.

There are no questions or objections.

The witness does not claim expenses.

Counsel for the plaintiff explains the request that the court dissolve the marriage through fault, taking the view that the fact that the defendant fathered an extramarital child during the marriage points to his culpable conduct, because this led to the disturbance of relations in the marriage. ### WITNESS KOPLJAN ROŽIKA, that is ROŽA
Daughter of father Pavle, born in 1955 in Novi Sad, housewife, completed eight grades of school, from Novi Sad, Zadruga 35, states:

As far as I know, my parents have not been getting along for a long time, somewhere around 7–8 years back. I know that there were disagreements about the education of the female children, because my father was against schooling and my mother was in favor of schooling. It has been at least six years since they separated. My mother ran the grain mill and my father managed the garden. They separated economically; each had his or her own income and expenses and managed them independently. My impression is that this was first because of taxes, so that everyone would know what belonged to whom, and that later it became a habit. They lived together formally. They borrowed money from each other, depending on who needed it.

I know that the plaintiff demanded money, that is income from the garden, and that the defendant did not bring it because the garden often did not yield, so he brought only produce, which was not enough for the plaintiff.

Their intolerance toward each other is deep and, as I heard from them, dates back to the very first year of the marriage, which they nevertheless maintained because of the children.

I know that the defendant had a child with a female laborer. He told me about that child and about that relationship with the laborer. He told me that he had been forced to live with her because he had no one to help him on the farmstead, especially since he was ill. He came to love the child. The child is in my care. He pays me 1,000 dinars per month. From time to time he brings fruit, vegetables, and meat.

While the defendant was on the farmstead, the plaintiff did not cook for him or bring him food. I left the house five years ago. ## Reasoning

In the lawsuit of 16 November 1976, the plaintiff stated that in the long-standing marriage there had been alienation between the spouses, especially because they had separated economically and spatially, with the defendant cultivating the land and the plaintiff conducting the business of the grain mill; that the defendant had begun living with another woman, with whom he had fathered a child; and she therefore sought that the court dissolve the marriage through the exclusive fault of the defendant. As regards maintenance of the child—the minor Ištvan—she specified her claim at the main-hearing session of 6 October 1977. In the lawsuit she also requested that the court order the defendant to pay the litigation costs.

The defendant did not oppose the divorce, but sought that the court find mutual fault, because the plaintiff was also to blame for the breakdown of the marriage. He agreed regarding the maintenance of the minor Ištvan after the clarified claim of 6 October 1977.

Reconciliation of the spouses was unsuccessful, because the parties are categorical in demanding that the marriage be dissolved, especially since they are separated and the defendant has an extramarital child.

The court conducted the evidentiary proceedings by reading the extract from the marriage register of the People's Committee of the City of Senta no. 231/1951; the extract from the birth register of the Municipality of Novi Sad no. 1595/62 of 3 August 1962; the certificate of the Primary School “Svetozar Marković-Toza,” Novi Sad, of 3 November 1976; the certificate of the Novi Sad Municipal Assembly no. 05-437/2-1317 of 23 December 1975; by hearing the parties; by hearing the witnesses Mojsinov Spasa, Dimić Eržebet, Miletić Cvita, Mijatović Đorđe, Fekete Franja, Bicok Margita; by taking requested testimony from Gabrić Etelka and Gabrić Nikola; and by hearing witness Kopljan Roža; and on the basis of this evidentiary proceedings and the facts undisputed between the parties, it established the following FACTUAL STATE:

The parties concluded marriage before the registrar of the People's Committee of the City of Senta on 17 November 1951. Three children were born in the marriage, two now of full age; the minor Lakatoš Ištvan was born on 3 August 1962.

From the very beginning of the marriage, disagreements arose because of the different natures of the parties. The plaintiff is wilful, inclined to giving orders, a stronger personality, sexually uninterested, and did not tolerate the defendant's occasional drunkenness—which at first was even amusing to her—but later developed into alcoholism. The defendant was treated for alcoholism, without success, and all this led to quarrels and the separation of the spouses, so that the defendant increasingly withdrew into himself and cheated on the plaintiff with other women, which she did not tolerate. She insulted him, calling him a peasant (pejoratively), physically attacked him, and broke his nose. Such relations also led to economic separation. Go...
