### IV. CONCISE LEGAL BASIS (FAMILY-LAW ASPECT — 1976)

— Articles 2, 5, 10, 14, 15, 30, 31 and 41 of the SAP Vojvodina Adoption Act (1976): full adoption may be established only if the prerequisites are fulfilled (no later than the age of five, and, if the parents are known and alive, with their consent), following a preparatory procedure and exclusively in the child’s interests.

— The full-adoption decision is dated 21 June 1979, seven days before my fifth birthday and before the judicial order of 20 July 1979 (which ordered protection of inheritance rights, not adoption).

— The father acknowledged paternity in litigation, and the mother was alive and known; the defendant’s claims “father unknown/address unknown” are refuted by the divorce and probate files and civil registry documents.

— “Second adoption” (1984): this legal institution is not provided for after full adoption has already been established; any changes in status must proceed exclusively through annulment/setting aside, not through a new adoption. The adopter’s “consent” of 1984 is a legal anomaly and confirms a continuity of unlawful conduct.

## Editorial legal analysis — representation, the hearing record and written continuation

Prepared with AI assistance from the supplied documents and the ZPP applicable in October 2025. This analysis is separate from the plaintiff’s submission and the court’s findings.

**Branislav Čolak’s status requires a concrete check.** The 15 October attendance entry calls him a CSR law graduate whose power of attorney is being submitted to the file. That formula does not supply its receipt date, employment evidence or proof of the judicial examination. Article 85(4) permits a legal entity to act through an attorney or its employee who is a law graduate with the judicial examination passed. These statutory alternatives make the plaintiff’s request for status evidence concrete; an online namesake does not establish who represented CSR or that person’s status on the hearing date. [ZPP, Article 85(4), PDF page 16](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD%20%D0%BE%20%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%20%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%BA%D1%83%2010-23.pdf#page=16).

**Authority and later approval must both be examined.** Articles 90 and 91 require written authority at the first procedural action and continuing judicial control; Article 91 also expressly recognises the party’s subsequent approval of unauthorised actions. The proper inquiry is therefore the actual instrument, filing date, scope and any subsequent approval, rather than treating every action as automatically ineffective from the attendance wording alone. In Rev 2731/2022, the Supreme Court explained that general litigation authority is not invariably subject to certification and addressed approval of actions. That judgment concerns a different dispute, not Čolak’s actual authority. [ZPP, Articles 90–91, PDF page 18](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD%20%D0%BE%20%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%20%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%BA%D1%83%2010-23.pdf#page=18); [Rev 2731/2022, 31 March 2022](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sr/%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2-27312022-1641-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BE%D1%9B-%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%80-%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B5-%D0%B2%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8).

**The record must preserve what the plaintiff actually stated.** Article 118 gives parties the right to inspect or read the record, object to its contents, and have corrections or additions entered at the end; unaccepted objections are entered on request. The eight-day objection in Article 121(3) specifically concerns a material discrepancy between an audio recording and its written transcript. It must not be mechanically applied to every ordinary written record. Here “no objections to what was read” appears before the plaintiff’s examination and refers to the previously read case file. It is not a blanket acceptance of the later wording of his testimony. [ZPP, Article 118, PDF page 23](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD%20%D0%BE%20%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%20%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%BA%D1%83%2010-23.pdf#page=23); [Article 121, PDF page 24](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD%20%D0%BE%20%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%20%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%BA%D1%83%2010-23.pdf#page=24).

**The written statement and the later submission have distinct procedural roles.** The 15 October record expressly enters the written statement and records service of a copy on CSR, then grants fifteen days from announcement of the order for further evidentiary proposals. Under Article 103, the first counted day is the following day; that ordinary calendar calculation ends on 30 October. The present text is dated 27 October. Article 104 treats the actual registered-post submission date as the court-submission date, but the closing notation is not a postal receipt. The materials therefore establish the content and written date while leaving the actual dispatch/receipt record to be checked. [ZPP, Articles 103–104, PDF pages 20–21](https://www.vrh.sud.rs/sites/default/files/attachments/%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD%20%D0%BE%20%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%20%D0%BF%D0%BE%D1%81%D1%82%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%BA%D1%83%2010-23.pdf#page=20).
