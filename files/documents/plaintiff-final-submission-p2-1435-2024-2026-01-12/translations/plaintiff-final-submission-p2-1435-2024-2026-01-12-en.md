By the cited decision, the first-instance court determined the extent of the deceased’s estate and the circle of heirs and awarded each of the heirs — Kopljan Roža, Beker Irena, minor Lakatuš Ištvan and minor Miletić Milivoj — one quarter of the estate.

that the deceased merely intended to adopt him but did not carry out that intention, offering the reading of the competent Self-Managing Community of Interest for Social Welfare’s files as evidence of that circumstance.

The appeal is well-founded.

The basic condition for statutory inheritance concerns the degree of kinship between the deceased and the heir. A person claiming an inheritance or a share of it must first prove kinship with the deceased — that is, establish his standing as an heir. According to the death record, the deceased’s heirs are daughters Kopljan Roža and Beker Irena and son minor Lakatuš Ištvan. This is particularly so because the appeal asserts that the deceased was neither minor Milivoj’s biological father nor his adoptive parent.

In view of the foregoing, the second-instance court allowed the appeal and set aside the challenged portion of the decision.

## Editorial links to the sources

The submission is dated **12 January 2026** on pages 26 and 75. The supplied copy does not itself establish a filing or court-receipt date. The main text and the author’s notes in the annexes convey the plaintiff’s allegations, conclusions and requests; the quoted records are identified separately.

**Articles 7 and 40.** The main text cites Article 7 of the 1976 SAP Vojvodina Law on Adoption. [Article 40](/en/assets/10#md-L160), which prohibits dissolution of full adoption, is also relevant to that argument. These are distinct provisions: one governs adoption of one person by several people, with an exception for spouses, while the other concerns the indissolubility of full adoption. The author’s foundational argument remains the initial act’s nullity; the prohibition of dissolution additionally exposes the problem of subsequent consent even if the initial act were hypothetically treated as valid.

**International-law citation.** On page 24, the author invokes Article II(e) of the [Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide](https://www.un.org/en/genocide-prevention/1948-convention). Its child-transfer provision is read with the introductory requirement of intent to destroy a protected group, in whole or in part. The submission presents the author’s argument about a pattern of conduct and future international scrutiny.
